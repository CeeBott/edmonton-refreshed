    Brands and retailers we actively buy from

    The list below is the core of our buy zone. It isn't exhaustive — pieces from other recognized makers come up regularly — but it covers the bulk of what we acquire and resell. We've grouped by tier rather than alphabetically because that's how the offer math works.

    • Italian and European top tier: B&B Italia, Natuzzi Italia, Roche Bobois, Cassina, Minotti
    • Natuzzi Editions: the Saggezza, Indimenticabile, Cesare, Sollievo, and Vigore models all have established resale paths in our market
    • American premium: American Leather, Restoration Hardware (particularly The Cloud, Maxwell, and Petite Maxwell), Room & Board, Design Within Reach
    • Canadian mid-to-upper: Rove Concepts (Milo, Aiden, Jagger), EQ3 (Replay, Remi, Reverie, Salema), Brentwood Classics, Younger Furniture, VanGogh, Decor-Rest
    • Catalogue retailers (select pieces): Crate & Barrel (Lounge, Gather, Rochelle), West Elm (Hamilton, Harmony, Andes — in leather or performance fabric), Pottery Barn, Urban Barn (Renfrew, Karis, Preston), CB2, Article (select)
    • La-Z-Boy (selectively): the leather and premium upholstered lines — not the standard recliner inventory
    • Unbranded with provenance: pieces originally purchased from premium Edmonton retailers like Signature Lane or Cottswood Interiors are evaluated on the same terms even when the manufacturer isn't obvious

    What affects the offer on a designer piece

    Three inputs drive the number on every offer: brand tier, age, and condition. On designer pieces specifically, a few additional factors come into play:

    • Leather grade: top-grain, semi-aniline, and full-aniline rank above split-leather or bonded variants. The difference is meaningful — often the gap between a strong offer and a weak one
    • Fabric category: performance fabrics from premium retailers hold value well; pale bouclé and velvet on mid-tier brands are more condition-dependent
    • Configuration: complete modular sets in their original configuration outperform partials or reconfigured pieces. Sectionals with all original modules and accessories (chaise, ottoman if matching) carry stronger numbers
    • Cushion condition: compressed seat cushions are common on older pieces, especially on Editions models with non-removable inserts. We factor this in transparently
    • Provenance: original receipts, warranty documents, or order confirmations from the retailer help confirm model and finish, particularly on Italian pieces where finish details affect value significantly

    Where Marketplace and consignment compete with us

    For genuinely high-end pieces in excellent condition with neutral upholstery, both Marketplace and consignment can outperform our offer on the final number. Marketplace gives you direct buyer pricing without intermediaries; consignment gives a designer piece a storefront with foot traffic that finds the right buyer. Either can produce a higher result than a direct buyout if the piece, the price, the timing, and the buyer pool align.

    What both require is patience. Marketplace works over weeks of messaging and showings. Consignment works over 60 to 90 days plus a 40 to 50 percent commission on the eventual sale. Both require you to coordinate transport (in or out, depending on the channel). We've covered the consignment math more fully on the consignment page.

    Our offer trades some final-number flexibility for speed, certainty, and pickup handled. For sellers who'd rather not run a sales process or wait through a consignment cycle on a designer piece, the trade tends to be worth it.

    What we don't typically buy

    Mass-market pieces (Ashley, IKEA, Leon's, The Brick), bonded or faux leather, microfiber recliners, hide-a-beds with mechanical issues, and items with significant pet or stain damage fall outside our category — even when they originally retailed at high prices. The pre-owned market doesn't support those at the margins that make a fair offer possible. For those categories, classifieds or donation through Find Edmonton Furniture Bank, the Bissell Centre, or Habitat for Humanity ReStore is usually the more honest path.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    What counts as a designer piece for your purposes?

    We use the term broadly to mean recognized design-oriented and premium retailers — Italian and European brands (B&B Italia, Natuzzi Italia, Natuzzi Editions, Roche Bobois), American premium (American Leather, Restoration Hardware, Room & Board), Canadian mid-to-upper (Rove Concepts, EQ3, Brentwood Classics, Younger Furniture, VanGogh), and select catalogue retailers (Crate & Barrel, West Elm, Pottery Barn, Urban Barn). If your piece came from one of those names, or from a premium retailer like Signature Lane or Cottswood Interiors, it's worth sending in.

    Does fabric or leather make a meaningful difference?

    Yes. On designer pieces, top-grain, semi-aniline, and full-aniline leathers tend to hold the strongest pre-owned values. Performance fabrics from premium retailers also resell well. Lighter fabrics, bouclé in pale colourways, and velvet on mid-tier brands are more variable — often acceptable, but with more weight on condition.

    How old is too old for a designer piece?

    Less than you'd think. Designer pieces from recognized makers were generally built to last decades, and the secondary market for them is healthier than for newer mass-market equivalents. A well-maintained 12-year-old Natuzzi Italia or B&B Italia piece is often a stronger buy than a 3-year-old volume-brand equivalent. Send photos and we'll give you a straight read.

    Do you buy designer accent chairs or only sofas and sectionals?

    We focus on sofas and sectionals. Matching designer accent chairs sold as part of a sofa-and-chair set are typically in. Standalone designer chairs — even from strong brands — are usually outside what we buy because our resale channels are tuned for primary seating pieces.

    I have proof of original purchase from a premium retailer. Does that help?

    Yes. Original receipts, warranty documents, or fabric/finish specifications from the retailer's order confirmation help us evaluate the piece quickly and confidently. They're not required, but they speed things along — particularly on pieces where the model name isn't obvious from photos.

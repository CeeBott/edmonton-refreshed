    Why Crate & Barrel pieces move

    Crate & Barrel sits in the upper-middle of the North American retail market — well-built hardwood frames, sinuous spring bases on standard models and higher-tier construction on the better configurations, and a recognisable design language. Buyers searching for a quality replacement piece often start here because they've owned one before or know someone who has.

    The strongest pre-owned models we're actively buying are The Gather, especially the deeper configurations, the Lounge, and the Axis. Leather configurations across any of these lines are consistently in demand and tend to price well. CB2 sister-brand pieces are also considered on a case-by-case basis.

    C&B fabrics tend to be durable, but lighter neutral upholstery shows wear faster than darker or patterned variants. Performance fabric and leather configurations hold up best over time and price strongest at resale — if your piece is in either, that works in your favour.

    Pieces we buy

    • Lounge, Axis, and Gather sofas and sectionals
    • CB2 sectionals
    • Leather configurations of any model
    • Performance-fabric and slipcovered pieces

    Does Crate & Barrel buy back or take trade-ins?

    No. Crate & Barrel doesn’t run a buy-back or trade-in program for used furniture. Its Trade Program is a discount for interior designers and other professionals buying new, not a way to hand back an old sofa. When a new Gather or Lounge is on order, the outgoing piece is still yours to deal with.

    Selling it to us is the closest thing to a trade-in. Send photos before the new piece arrives and tell us the delivery date. If you accept our offer, we can usually time pickup so the old sofa is gone first, and you’re paid by cash or e-transfer rather than store credit, so the money can go toward the new piece or anywhere else.

    Why Edmonton sellers contact us about a Crate & Barrel piece

    The piece is great but the room changed

    Renovation, new flooring, a repaint, or a full design refresh. The Crate & Barrel piece is in excellent shape; the direction the room is going simply calls for something different. A design change is one of the most common reasons a well-maintained designer piece moves on.

    New furniture is on the way

    Upgrading your living room means the existing sofa or sectional needs to be gone before — or by — a specific date. Sellers contact us to schedule a pickup around their delivery window rather than manage an open-ended Marketplace listing during the same week. For sellers hoping to trade in the outgoing sofa when the new one arrives, this is the practical equivalent — the old piece bought outright, gone on a date you set.

    Downsizing to a space where it no longer fits

    A family home giving way to a condo, townhouse, or suite often means quality furniture that won’t clear the new floor plan — one of the most common reasons sellers reach out before a move. The Gather and Lounge are generously proportioned by design — a feature in a large living room and a logistics problem in a smaller one.

    No interest in running the Marketplace process

    Photographing, listing, answering questions, vetting buyers, scheduling walkthroughs, and coordinating a large-furniture pickup with a stranger takes time that not everyone has. Sellers with good pieces who’d rather trade some of the ceiling for certainty and a single conversation are the ones we hear from most.

    What affects the offer
    • Model: Lounge, Axis, and Gather lead the line in resale value
    • Configuration: deep sofas and modular sectionals with all original components outperform stripped or partial configurations
    • Upholstery: performance fabric and leather hold value better than light linens or velvets
    • Cushion state: C&B deep sofas are known for generous cushioning that can compress over time; shape retention matters
    • Age: 0–6 years is the strongest band; well-maintained older pieces still considered if the frame is solid and quiet

    How to identify your Crate & Barrel model

    Crate & Barrel rotates models over time, with multiple generations of the same name (Lounge I, II, Deep; Axis I, II) and variants in slipcovered vs. upholstered builds. A few practical ways to identify what you have for a more accurate offer.

    • Check the product tag. Usually stapled under a seat cushion or stitched into a slipcover seam. The tag often spells out the model name and production date — both let us match to the right generation.
    • Photograph the silhouette from the side. Cushion depth, arm style, and back height changed between generations. A side-on photo lets us match to current or recent catalogue images.
    • Note the leg style and finish. Lounge generations have slightly different leg shapes (tapered vs. block) and finishes (walnut, natural, weathered) that help pin down the production year.
    • Confirm slipcovered vs. upholstered. Slipcovered models have removable, washable covers; upholstered models have fixed fabric. The retail price and our offer differ between the two — clarify which yours is.
    • Pull any original purchase documents. C&B emails the order confirmation with model name and fabric code; many buyers keep these in inbox archives.

    If you can’t tell, send photos of the side profile, the leg style, the cushion top, and the tag. We match to the right generation in most cases.

    Selling a Crate & Barrel piece to us vs. listing on Marketplace

    Crate & Barrel has an Edmonton location, which means many local buyers already know the brand and search for it by name on the resale market. Marketplace works reasonably well for C&B pieces with strong photos and time to wait. What we offer is a different trade — certainty, scheduled pickup, and a single conversation. The C&B Lounge in particular is large and heavy enough that pickup logistics are worth weighing.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    How quickly will I hear back?

    We aim to reply the same day we receive your photos, and we’ll arrange a time that works for you.

    Do you buy older Crate & Barrel pieces?

    Yes. Crate & Barrel frames hold up well, and we look at fabric and structural condition more than the year of purchase. Age matters less than how the piece has been maintained.

    Do you take CB2 too?

    Yes — contemporary CB2 sectionals are part of our buy stream alongside main-brand Crate & Barrel.

    What about Crate & Barrel slipcovered sofas?

    Worth sending. Slipcovers that are intact and clean are a feature, not a flaw — many buyers prefer them.

    Where can I sell my Crate & Barrel sofa in Edmonton?

    Edmonton Refreshed buys Crate & Barrel sofas and sectionals directly from sellers across Edmonton, St. Albert, Sherwood Park, Spruce Grove, Leduc, and Fort Saskatchewan, including the Lounge, Axis, Gather, and Rochelle. Send photos through the form on this page. We aim to reply the same day.

    Is there a Crate & Barrel buy-back or trade-in program in Edmonton?

    No — Crate & Barrel doesn’t offer a buy-back or trade-in program. But you can sell yours outright to Edmonton Refreshed — send photos for an offer, paid by cash or e-transfer with pickup handled.

    Will I get more for my Crate & Barrel piece on Marketplace?

    Possibly — the Lounge and Axis lines have strong Marketplace performance because Edmonton buyers know the brand and the models. The honest trade is that with time, a strong listing on a well-maintained C&B piece can outperform our offer. What you give up is the time, the staging, the buyer logistics, and the size of moving an oversized C&B Lounge through a stairwell.

    Do you take the larger Crate & Barrel Lounge sectionals?

    Yes — the Lounge II in particular is one of the more consistent pieces we buy. Larger configurations are evaluated on condition and footprint, but size alone isn't a barrier. Tell us the configuration and dimensions on submission and we'll plan pickup logistics around the layout of your home.

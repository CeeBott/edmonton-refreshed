What We Buy in Edmonton (and What We Don’t)

Updated October 2026

Edmonton Refreshed buys pre-owned sofas and sectionals outright, primarily from premium brands, ideally within about ten years old and in Good condition or better. We don’t buy mass-market pieces or bonded leather, because the resale market for them doesn’t support a fair offer. This page is the honest filter: check your piece against the lists below before you send photos.

If it doesn’t clear the list, we’ll point you to a better option. No hard feelings.

Check Your Piece

Before you send photos

We buy pieces that are all of these

  • A sofa or sectional (exceptional chairs considered)
  • From a recognized brand or premium retailer ($3,000+ new)
  • Roughly 10 years old or newer
  • In good condition (no major damage, staining, or pet damage)

We don’t make offers on

  • IKEA, Ashley, Leon’s, The Brick, or similar
  • Bonded or faux leather, or microfiber
  • Tables, desks, storage, or bedroom furniture

How our offers work

We buy to resell, so every offer factors in what it costs us to transport, clean, store, and remarket a piece. That means our offer will come in below what a private sale would net you. If maximizing price is the priority, a private sale is the better option. What we offer instead is certainty: a firm number, paid before the piece leaves your home, with no listing, no messaging, and no no-shows. If that trade is worth it to you, we’re a good option.

    What affects the offer

    Three inputs drive every offer: brand tier, age, and condition. Within those, a handful of details move the number up or down — and we explain them transparently when we send an offer rather than burying them:

    • Leather grade: top-grain, semi-aniline, and full-aniline rank well above split or bonded leather. On premium pieces this is often the single biggest factor
    • Fabric category: performance fabrics from premium retailers hold value; pale bouclé and velvet on mid-tier brands depend heavily on condition
    • Configuration: complete modular sets in their original layout outperform partials or reconfigured pieces; all original modules and accessories matter
    • Cushion condition: compressed seat cushions are common on older pieces, especially non-removable inserts — we factor this in honestly
    • Colourway: neutral upholstery resells faster than bold or polarizing colours, which narrow the buyer pool and lengthen resale time
    • Provenance: original receipts, warranty documents, or finish codes help confirm model and finish, particularly on Italian pieces

    We never publish per-piece price tables, because the honest number depends on all of the above together — and because a published figure just becomes a starting point that helps no one. Send photos and we give you a real number, not a range to argue over.

    Pieces we’ve passed on — and why

    Saying no is part of doing this honestly. These are real categories of pieces we’ve turned down, and what we suggested instead — because the right answer for your piece sometimes isn’t us.

    • A nearly-new mass-market sectional in great shape. Genuinely good condition, but from a volume brand. We passed because the pre-owned market for these is thin and a Facebook Marketplace listing would net the seller more than we could fairly offer. We told them to list it themselves.
    • A genuine premium piece with cracked, sun-faded leather. A top-tier brand, but the aniline leather on one arm had dried and split. Restoring premium leather is costly, and the condition erodes the resale margin past the point of a fair offer. We suggested an honest Marketplace listing or a design-focused consignment shop that takes pieces as-is.
    • A standalone designer accent chair from a brand we love. Excellent piece, wrong category — our resale channel is built for primary seating, not single chairs. We pointed the seller to a consignment shop that handles individual designer chairs well.
    • A strong-brand sofa in a bold, polarizing colourway. Recognized maker, good condition, but a colour that suits a narrow slice of buyers. Resale time runs long on pieces like this, so Marketplace to exactly the right buyer usually beats us. We made a modest offer and told them to try listing it first.

    If your piece is in one of these categories, that doesn’t mean it has no value — it means we may not be the best channel for it. We’ll say so plainly, and point you somewhere better.

    Where to take pieces we don’t buy

    For decent mass-market pieces in good shape, Facebook Marketplace and Kijiji give you the largest audience and the best price — list them yourself. For pieces past their prime, donation keeps them out of the landfill and helps someone locally: Find Edmonton Furniture Bank, the Bissell Centre, and Habitat for Humanity ReStore all accept furniture in usable condition. We’re glad to point you the right way even when we’re not the buyer.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    How do I know if my piece is something you’d buy?

    The quick filter is three things: it’s a sofa or sectional, it came from a recognized premium or design-oriented brand, and it’s in Good condition or better. If all three are true, it’s very likely a fit. If you’re only sure of one or two, send photos anyway — we give every submission a straight read, and the worst case is a fast, honest no.

    What if my brand isn’t on your list?

    The list isn’t exhaustive. We buy sofas and sectionals from many recognized makers beyond the ones named here, plus well-built unbranded pieces from premium Edmonton retailers like Signature Lane or Cottswood Interiors. If your piece is a quality sofa or sectional and you’re unsure where it lands, send it in — naming the original retailer in the notes helps us evaluate it quickly.

    Do you buy anything other than sofas and sectionals?

    Primarily sofas and sectionals — that’s what our resale channels are built for. Matching accent chairs or ottomans sold as part of a sofa set are typically in. Standalone chairs, dining sets, bedroom furniture, and office furniture are outside what we buy, even from strong brands.

    My piece is high-end but has some wear. Is it worth sending?

    Usually yes. Premium pieces from recognized makers tolerate age better than mass-market equivalents, and the secondary market for them is healthier. Wear affects the offer, but it rarely rules a strong piece out on its own. The exceptions are cracked or peeling leather, deep staining, smoke saturation, and structural damage — send photos and we’ll tell you honestly where it lands.

    What should I do with a piece you don’t buy?

    For decent mass-market pieces in good shape, Facebook Marketplace or Kijiji will almost always net you more than we fairly could — list it yourself. For pieces past their prime, donation through Find Edmonton Furniture Bank, the Bissell Centre, or Habitat for Humanity ReStore is the more honest path. We’re happy to point you in the right direction even when the answer isn’t us.

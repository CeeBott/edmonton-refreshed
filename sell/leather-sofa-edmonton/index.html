Edmonton Refreshed buys quality pre-owned leather sofas outright in Edmonton. Send photos for a no-obligation offer within about 24 hours, then we arrange pickup and pay on the spot. Genuine top-grain leather from recognized brands is one of the categories we look for most.

Why Edmonton sellers contact us about a leather sofa

Marketplace buyers can’t identify the grade

Aniline, semi-aniline, and top-grain look different at viewings, and buyers who don’t know often lowball or back out at the door. Sellers come to us when they want the grade identified up front and priced accordingly.

Replacing the leather with a different look

Renovating, refreshing the room, or moving to a different upholstery direction (fabric, sectional, smaller piece). The old leather has real value but doesn’t fit the next chapter.

Lifestyle no longer suits leather

New kids, pets, allergies, or a colour-palette shift. The piece is fine; the household just needs something more forgiving.

A second leather piece going at the same time

Matching loveseat, chair set, or companion sectional. We can take the qualifying pieces in a single trip.

    Why we're always in the market for leather sofas

    A new top-grain leather sofa from a recognized brand starts around $2,500 CAD and runs past $8,000 for premium models. Buyers at that price point tend to know that a well-maintained pre-owned piece often represents better value than a new entry-tier sofa — the same money buys more sofa on the secondary market.

    Some of the brands we focus on for leather: Natuzzi Italia and Editions, B&B Italia, American Leather, Brentwood Classics (especially the Dorado in full-aniline), Restoration Hardware, Crate & Barrel leather lines, Urban Barn (top-grain only), and EQ3 (Remi). La-Z-Boy leather is accepted selectively at the higher-end models.

    On condition: minor surface wear, light patina, and small scuffs are usually fine — in many cases they're what buyers actively prefer over an overly protected, plasticky-looking finish. What does affect offers: cracking, peeling, significant staining, ink marks, and pet damage. Photos let us evaluate accurately.

    What affects the offer

    • Leather grade: full-aniline and semi-aniline lead; top-grain is the floor; bonded and faux leather are generally outside our scope
    • Brand: premium European, American, and Canadian manufacturers set the offer range
    • Colour: cognac, espresso, charcoal, and black are the strongest resale colours; bright or saturated hues narrow the buyer pool
    • Surface condition: minor wear is acceptable; significant cracking or peeling reduces offers materially
    • Frame and cushion: hardwood frames with intact spring systems and shape-retaining cushions support the strongest offers

    How to identify your leather sofa’s grade

    A few practical checks tell you where on the leather spectrum your sofa sits — useful for setting expectations on the offer range.

    • Surface up close. Full-aniline and semi-aniline leathers have natural variation: small marks, subtle colour shifts, and a soft, slightly matte hand. Top-grain has a more uniform finish — protected against staining but with less character. Bonded or split-hide leather often looks plasticky and starts peeling along seat edges as it ages.
    • Temperature to the touch. Aniline leathers warm to skin temperature quickly. Heavily coated or bonded leathers stay cool because they’re effectively wrapped in a polymer film.
    • The manufacturer tag. Usually under a seat cushion or stapled to the underside of the frame. Tags often spell out the grade ("top-grain leather", "semi-aniline", "Categoria 25"). Take a clear photo of the tag and include it.
    • Smell. Real leather has a distinct, earthy smell. Bonded or PU-coated leather has a chemical or plastic-y smell, especially when newer.
    • Seam behaviour. Aniline and semi-aniline leathers soften at the seams gradually — a worn but intact edge after years of use. Bonded leather cracks or peels at the seams, often within five to eight years of regular use.

    If you can’t tell, send photos of the surface, edges, and any visible tag. We identify the grade from photos in most cases and quote accordingly.

    Leather sofa types we look for

    • Top-grain or higher from Natuzzi, B&B Italia, American Leather, Restoration Hardware, and Brentwood Classics
    • Full-aniline leather sofas in any size or configuration — we actively look for these
    • Loveseat and chair sets in matching leather — we prefer to take these together with the sofa
    • Well-maintained leather sofas showing natural patina — light surface wear and honest aging are not barriers
    • Two and three-seat leather sofas as well as larger configurations — size doesn't limit eligibility if the piece and brand qualify

    Leather sofas we don’t typically purchase

    • Bonded leather or split-hide constructions, including peeling or cracking configurations
    • Pieces labelled simply "genuine leather" (the lowest grade — tends to crack and peel within years)
    • Faux leather, PU-coated, or microfiber upholstery
    • Mass-market pieces from Ashley, IKEA, Leon's, or The Brick
    • Significant cracking, ink marks, deep scratching, or pet damage
    • Heavy smoke saturation or embedded odour

    For pieces in those categories, donation through Find Edmonton Furniture Bank, the Bissell Centre, or Habitat for Humanity ReStore is usually the better path. If you’re unsure, send photos — leather grade is often clearer in good photos than the original label suggests.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    My leather is faded on one side from the sun — is the piece still worth anything?

    Usually yes, but with a reduced offer that reflects the fading. Send photos and we'll give you a straight read on how much it matters.

    Should I condition the leather before selling?

    Not necessarily. We clean and inspect everything we take in. Applying a conditioner to aged leather right before a sale can cause uneven absorption or surface reactions — better to leave it as-is and let us assess it fresh.

    What's the difference between "genuine leather" and "real leather"?

    Both terms refer to actual animal hide — but "genuine leather" is a specific grade, and the lowest one. It's made from the split layer of the hide and tends to wear and peel faster than top-grain. Top-grain and full-grain are the grades that hold their value on the secondary market. If your sofa is labelled genuine leather, it's worth sending photos, but it's a harder buy for us.

    My sofa is leather on the seating surfaces but vinyl on the sides and back — does it qualify?

    Sometimes. Split-hide construction — leather on contact areas, vinyl elsewhere — is common at mid-tier price points. We evaluate these case by case based on brand and overall condition.

    Where can I sell my leather sofa in Edmonton?

    Edmonton Refreshed buys leather sofas directly from sellers across Edmonton, St. Albert, Sherwood Park, Spruce Grove, Leduc, and Fort Saskatchewan — aniline, semi-aniline, and top-grain leather configurations from recognized brands. Submit photos through the form on this page and we'll reply with a firm offer within 24 hours, with the leather grade identified up front.

    Will I get more for my leather sofa on Marketplace?

    Possibly — leather sofas are one of the stronger Marketplace categories. The honest trade is that with the right buyer, the right colour, and time to wait, a Marketplace listing on a quality leather sofa can outperform our offer. Our offer trades some price for certainty, leather-grade identification done up front, and pickup logistics handled by people who move leather pieces routinely.

    How do you identify leather grade from photos?

    Close-up photos of the surface, edges, and seams let us identify the grade in most cases. Aniline and semi-aniline leathers have natural variation and absorb light differently than top-grain or corrected-grain — the differences are visible in good photos. If the original purchase documents specify the grade, those help too.

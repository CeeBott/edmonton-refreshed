Sell Your Leather Sofa in Edmonton

Pre-owned leather sofas from quality manufacturers are one of the most consistent categories we buy. Leather ages slowly, holds its structure well, and draws steady buyer demand in Edmonton year-round — more so than most fabric upholstery.

Get an Offer

Why leather sofas resell so well

A new top-grain leather sofa from a recognized brand starts around $2,500 CAD and runs past $8,000 for premium models. Buyers at that price point tend to know that a well-maintained pre-owned piece often represents better value than a new entry-tier sofa — the same money buys more sofa on the secondary market.

Some of the brands we focus on for leather: Natuzzi Italia and Editions, B&B Italia, American Leather, Brentwood Classics (especially the Dorado in full-aniline), Restoration Hardware, Crate & Barrel leather lines, Urban Barn (top-grain only), and EQ3 (Remi). La-Z-Boy leather is accepted selectively at the higher-end models.

On condition: minor surface wear, light patina, and small scuffs are usually fine — in many cases they're what buyers actively prefer over an overly protected, plasticky-looking finish. What does affect offers: cracking, peeling, significant staining, ink marks, and pet damage. Photos let us evaluate accurately.

What affects the offer

  • Leather grade: full-aniline and semi-aniline lead; top-grain is the floor; bonded and faux leather are generally outside our scope
  • Brand: premium European, American, and Canadian manufacturers set the offer range
  • Colour: cognac, espresso, charcoal, and black are the strongest resale colours; bright or saturated hues narrow the buyer pool
  • Surface condition: minor wear is acceptable; significant cracking or peeling reduces offers materially
  • Frame and cushion: hardwood frames with intact spring systems and shape-retaining cushions support the strongest offers

Leather sofa types we look for

  • Top-grain or higher from Natuzzi, B&B Italia, American Leather, Restoration Hardware, and Brentwood Classics
  • Full-aniline leather sofas in any size or configuration — we actively look for these
  • Loveseat and chair sets in matching leather — we prefer to take these together with the sofa
  • Well-maintained leather sofas showing natural patina — light surface wear and honest aging are not barriers
  • Two and three-seat leather sofas as well as larger configurations — size doesn't limit eligibility if the piece and brand qualify

    Frequently Asked Questions

    My leather is faded on one side from the sun — is the piece still worth anything?

    Usually yes, but with a reduced offer that reflects the fading. Send photos and we'll give you a straight read on how much it matters.

    Should I condition the leather before selling?

    Not necessarily. We clean and inspect everything we take in. Applying a conditioner to aged leather right before a sale can cause uneven absorption or surface reactions — better to leave it as-is and let us assess it fresh.

    What's the difference between "genuine leather" and "real leather"?

    Both terms refer to actual animal hide — but "genuine leather" is a specific grade, and the lowest one. It's made from the split layer of the hide and tends to wear and peel faster than top-grain. Top-grain and full-grain are the grades that hold their value on the secondary market. If your sofa is labelled genuine leather, it's worth sending photos, but it's a harder buy for us.

    My sofa is leather on the seating surfaces but vinyl on the sides and back — does it qualify?

    Sometimes. Split-hide construction — leather on contact areas, vinyl elsewhere — is common at mid-tier price points. We evaluate these case by case based on brand and overall condition.

How our offers work

We buy to resell, so every offer factors in what it costs us to transport, clean, store, and remarket a piece. That means our offer will come in below what a private sale would net you. If maximizing price is the priority, a private sale is the better option. What we offer instead is certainty: a firm number, paid before the piece leaves your home, with no listing, no messaging, and no no-shows. If that trade is worth it to you, we’re a good option.

    What we look for from Pottery Barn

    Pottery Barn is part of Williams-Sonoma, Inc., the same company as West Elm. The Turner we bought was upholstered in Keystone, a top-grain, aniline-dyed leather, and built to contract grade, meaning it’s engineered to select ANSI/BIFMA commercial testing protocols, with no-sag sinuous steel springs.

    PB Comfort pieces are built on kiln-dried solid wood frames with mortise-and-tenon joinery and corner blocks. The York collection is built at Pottery Barn’s Sutter Street shop in Hickory, North Carolina.

    Pottery Barn comes through less often than the Italian leather brands, so we look at each piece on its own.

    Pieces we buy

    • Leather sofas and sectionals, like the Turner
    • PB Comfort and York sofas and sectionals
    • Fabric and slipcovered pieces
    • Sectionals and matching sets

    What affects the offer
    • Collection, such as the Turner, PB Comfort, or York.
    • Upholstery: leather or fabric, and the fabric's colour.
    • Age and condition, with condition counting for more than the year.
    • Configuration, including matching pieces sold together.

    How to identify your Pottery Barn collection

    On the Turner we handled, the Pottery Barn label was sewn into the body of the sofa below the centre seat cushion. Lift the seat cushions and photograph any label or tag you find.

    The collection name is also on your original order confirmation or receipt, which is the fastest way for us to price the piece.

    Selling to us vs. listing on Marketplace

    Pottery Barn is a name buyers recognize, so a well-kept piece can sell privately, and for the right piece Marketplace can pay more than our offer, if you have the weeks to run the listing, answer messages, and host showings, and a buyer actually follows through. Deep sofas and sectionals are also heavy and awkward to move.

    Our offer trades some of that ceiling for certainty: one conversation, a firm number, pickup handled by our crew, and payment before the piece leaves. If your goal is the highest possible price and you don’t mind the time, Marketplace may be the better fit.

    How to triage furniture in an estate

    An estate clear-out usually involves four categories of pieces, and naming them up front helps the rest of the process move smoothly.

    • Family keepsakes. Identify which pieces siblings or family members want to keep before anything goes to a sale or donation channel. Sectionals, side tables, and accent pieces sometimes carry more sentimental weight than the obvious heirlooms.
    • Resale-value pieces. Sofas and sectionals from recognized brands often have meaningful resale value, even years out from purchase. These are the pieces we evaluate. Designer dining sets, china cabinets, and antiques are usually best routed to an estate-sale company that specializes in those categories.
    • Donate-able pieces. Functional furniture that isn't from a resale-friendly brand still has real value to households that need it. Find Edmonton Furniture Bank, the Bissell Centre, and Habitat for Humanity ReStore each accept different categories and can often arrange pickup for larger items.
    • Removal or disposal. Items with significant damage, contamination, or end-of-life wear are usually handled through a junk-removal service. A good estate-sale company can fold this into their process; otherwise services like JustJunk or 1-800-GOT-JUNK come to the property.

    You don't need to have this sorted before sending photos. If pieces could go either way, send them anyway — a direct read on which qualify for resale often helps the rest of the triage fall into place.

    What estate selling typically looks like

    The estates we work with vary widely. Sometimes it's a single sectional from a parent's downsized condo. Sometimes it's a full living room and family room set from a longtime family home. Sometimes it's coordinated through an executor working with several siblings, none of whom live in Edmonton. The common thread is that the seating pieces are part of a larger picture — the house is being sold, the contents are being sorted, and decisions are being made on a different cadence than a typical sale.

    We try not to add pressure to that. There's no countdown, no “this offer is good for 24 hours” tactic. Photos in, offer back, scheduled pickup at a time the family chooses. If the timeline is weeks, that's fine. If you need pickup before the property changes hands next Tuesday, we'll work toward that.

    Where direct purchase fits

    The advantages of a direct buyout for estate situations tend to be practical:

    • One coordinated pickup. A single visit for any qualifying seating pieces, rather than multiple Marketplace strangers walking through the home.
    • Truck and crew handled. No expectation that family members or an executor lift, disassemble, or transport anything.
    • Paid at the door. Cash or e-transfer to whichever party the executor or family designates — useful when the proceeds need to flow through the estate's accounts.
    • Clear answer on what we can take and what we can't. We won't pretend to buy something that isn't in our category, and we're happy to point you toward the right channel for everything else.

    Selling estate furniture to us vs. running an estate sale or listing on Marketplace

    An estate clear-out has more channels available than a typical seller does — full-service estate sale companies, auction houses, Marketplace, donation. Each has its place. What we offer fits in the middle: direct purchase of the seating pieces that have meaningful resale value, so those don't have to wait for a sale-day, get marked down at auction, or sit on Marketplace through a probate window.

    Where other channels usually fit better

    For a full-estate clear-out involving more than the seating pieces, an estate sale company or specialist removal service is almost always the right primary partner. They handle the bedroom sets, dining sets, china, kitchenware, garage contents, and the catalogue of items that comes with a lifetime of accumulation. We can fit inside that process — taking the qualifying sofas and sectionals while they handle the rest — or work after the estate sale concludes if anything didn't move.

    For pieces that don't have meaningful resale value (older mass-market pieces, items with significant wear), donation through Find Edmonton Furniture Bank, the Bissell Centre, or Habitat for Humanity ReStore is usually the most useful path. Each accepts different categories and can often arrange pickup for larger items. The pieces find their way to households that need them, and the executor doesn't pay for landfill disposal.

    What we typically purchase

    Sofas and sectionals from recognized design-oriented and premium retailers: B&B Italia, Natuzzi Italia, Natuzzi Editions, Rove Concepts, Crate & Barrel, EQ3, West Elm, Restoration Hardware, American Leather, Brentwood Classics, Younger Furniture, VanGogh, and a handful of others. Urban Barn and Pottery Barn are accepted on select pieces. La-Z-Boy is accepted selectively, particularly the leather and premium upholstered lines.

    Estate pieces from older, well-regarded Canadian and American makers are often interesting to us even when they aren't on a current retailer list — furniture from previous decades was frequently built better than today's equivalent. Solid hardwood frames, real top-grain leather, hand-tied springs. If you're unsure, send photos with whatever brand information is available (often a tag under a cushion or stamped inside a frame).

    Pieces we don’t typically purchase

    • Mass-market pieces from Ashley, IKEA, Leon's, or The Brick
    • Bonded leather or heavily peeling configurations
    • Highly traditional, ornate, or niche styles outside our resale focus
    • Sectionals with major frame or recliner failures
    • Heavy smoke saturation or significant pet odour issues
    • Large visible tears, severe leather cracking, or structural instability

    For those, the donation and removal channels above are the more honest path. If you're unsure where your piece falls, send photos. We'll tell you straight, and if it's not for us we'll point you at whichever channel makes more sense.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    Do you handle the full estate, or only specific pieces?

    We buy sofas and sectionals only — that's where our resale channels and pricing are dialed in. Bedroom sets, dining sets, china cabinets, and standalone chairs fall outside what we purchase. For a full-estate clear-out, an estate sale company or a charitable removal service is usually the better partner; we can handle the seating pieces inside that broader process.

    How quickly do we need to decide?

    There's no pressure on timing from our end. Some families coordinate over weeks; others want pickup the same week as a possession date. Send photos when you're ready and we'll work to whatever timeline you and the rest of the family are comfortable with.

    Do vintage or traditional pieces qualify?

    We focus primarily on modern, transitional, and design-oriented sofas and sectionals that align with current resale demand. Age alone is not the deciding factor — we’ve purchased older high-quality pieces from brands like Natuzzi or American Leather — but highly traditional, ornate, or niche styles are usually outside our resale focus regardless of condition.

    Can the offer be paid to the estate rather than to me personally?

    Yes. We can pay to whichever party the executor or family designates — cash or e-transfer at pickup. We'll work with the documentation needs of the estate as required.

    Where can I sell estate furniture in Edmonton?

    For sofas and sectionals from recognized brands, Edmonton Refreshed buys directly from estates and executors across Edmonton, St. Albert, Sherwood Park, Spruce Grove, Leduc, and Fort Saskatchewan. For full-household clear-outs that include bedroom sets, dining, and lifetime accumulations, a local estate-sale company is usually the right primary partner — we can fit inside that process for the seating pieces, or work after the estate sale concludes.

    Do you work with estate executors or only the family?

    Both. Many submissions come from adult children or estate executors managing a parent's home. We're comfortable with delayed timelines, probate considerations, multiple decision-makers, and family coordination — and the offer can be paid to whichever party the estate designates.

    What if the estate furniture isn't in great condition?

    We'll be straight with you. If the brand and structural condition support a fair offer, we'll make one. If the piece is past resale — significant wear, damaged frame, embedded odour — we'll say so, and usually point you to donation through Find Edmonton Furniture Bank, the Bissell Centre, or Habitat for Humanity ReStore, or a removal service for items at end of life.

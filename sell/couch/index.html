    What we look for

    We focus on couches from design-oriented and premium retailers: Italian and European brands (Natuzzi Italia, Natuzzi Editions, B&B Italia), American premium (American Leather, Restoration Hardware, Room & Board), Canadian mid-to-upper (Rove Concepts, EQ3, Brentwood Classics, Younger Furniture, Decor-Rest), and select catalogue retailers (Crate & Barrel, West Elm, Pottery Barn, Urban Barn).

    For each one, the same three inputs drive the offer: brand tier, age, and condition. A recent recognised-brand couch in excellent condition holds a meaningful share of its value pre-owned. An older couch from a mass-market brand sells for a fraction of original retail regardless of what it cost new. Mid-cases are evaluated on photos.

    What affects the offer

    • Brand tier: design-oriented and premium brands lead; mass-market brands are typically out of scope
    • Age: 0–6 years from a low-use household is the strongest band; well-maintained older pieces still considered if the frame is solid and quiet
    • Upholstery: leather and performance fabric outperform light linens and velvets
    • Cushion and frame: solid frame, intact springs, and shape-retaining cushions support stronger offers
    • Configuration: matching chairs or ottomans can strengthen the offer when sold with the primary couch

    How to evaluate your couch for resale

    A few quick checks tell you whether your couch is in the band where it's worth submitting.

    • Brand check. Is the brand on the manufacturer tag (usually under a cushion or stapled to the back of the frame) one of the design-oriented or premium retailers above? If yes, send photos. If it's Ashley, IKEA, Leon's, or The Brick, the answer is usually no.
    • Frame and structure. Sit on the couch and feel for movement in the frame. A solid hardwood frame holds value. A frame that creaks, shifts, or has visible joint issues is a meaningful deduction.
    • Cushions. Press into the seat — do the cushions hold their shape and rebound, or do they stay compressed? Non-removable cushions are harder to recover from compression.
    • Upholstery. Leather and performance fabric are easier to resell than light linens, velvets, or specialty fabrics. Check for cracking, peeling, fading, and embedded pet damage.
    • Smell. Air out the home, then walk in cold. Significant smoke or pet odour is hard to undo and affects what we can offer.

    If most of those check out, photograph the couch from multiple angles plus any wear, and submit through the form below. We'll give you a straight read the same day.

    Couch types we look for

    • Stationary couches from Natuzzi, B&B Italia, American Leather, Restoration Hardware, and Brentwood Classics
    • Leather couches in any premium grade.
    • Performance-fabric and wool-blend couches from EQ3, Rove Concepts, and Crate & Barrel.
    • Loveseats and matching chairs bundled with the primary couch
    • Couches from premium Edmonton retailers like Cottswood Interiors F2, or Signature Lane

    Couches we don’t typically purchase

    • Mass-market couches from Ashley, IKEA, Leon's, or The Brick
    • Bonded leather or heavily peeling configurations
    • Couches with major frame issues or recliner failures
    • Heavy smoke saturation or significant pet odour issues
    • Large visible tears, severe leather cracking, or structural instability

    If your couch falls into any of these categories, donation through Find Edmonton Furniture Bank, the Bissell Centre, or Habitat for Humanity ReStore is usually the better path. If you're unsure, send photos — we'll tell you straight.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    My couch is from Ashley, IKEA, or a similar store — is it worth sending photos?

    Probably not. We focus on design-oriented and premium brands because the Edmonton resale market doesn't support the numbers on mass-market pieces. If you're not sure whether your brand qualifies, send a photo anyway and we'll give you a straight answer.

    Will you take a 10+ year old couch?

    Sometimes — brand and condition carry more weight than age once a piece is past our typical window. Older couches from premium brands can still hold meaningful resale value. Photos tell us more than the purchase date does.

    My couch has a tear in one cushion — is that a deal-breaker?

    Not always. A small tear in an otherwise solid piece factors into the offer but doesn't usually disqualify it. Send photos and we'll give you a straight answer.

    What happens after I send photos?

    If the piece looks like a fit we'll follow up with an offer, usually the same day. If we move forward, we arrange pickup at a time that works for you and handle everything from there.

    Where can I sell my couch in Edmonton?

    Edmonton Refreshed buys quality couches directly from sellers across Edmonton, St. Albert, Sherwood Park, Spruce Grove, Leduc, and Fort Saskatchewan — same criteria as a sofa, since they're the same piece. Submit photos through the form on this page and we'll get you an offer today. Facebook Marketplace and Kijiji are the other direct-sale channels, with the trade-off of running the listing process yourself.

    Who buys used couches in Edmonton?

    Edmonton Refreshed focuses specifically on sofas and sectionals from recognized brands — Natuzzi, B&B Italia, Rove Concepts, EQ3, Crate & Barrel, West Elm, American Leather, Restoration Hardware, Brentwood Classics, Pottery Barn, Urban Barn, and similar. We're one of the few buyers in Edmonton focused on the couch category specifically.

    Is a couch the same thing as a sofa for selling purposes?

    Yes — same piece, same evaluation criteria, same offer process. We use the words interchangeably and accept submissions under either label. The difference is mostly regional vocabulary, not anything structural.

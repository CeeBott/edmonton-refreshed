    Leather grades, in practice

    Not all leather is equal — and the grade has a meaningful effect on resale value. From highest to entry-level: full-aniline (undyed, develops genuine patina, no surface coating), semi-aniline (light protective coat, near-top quality), top-grain (the standard at premium brands — durable, slightly less natural in hand), then corrected-grain and split leather (entry-tier, less resaleable). We focus on top-grain and above.

    Brand matters too. Natuzzi Italia, B&B Italia, American Leather, and Restoration Hardware lead our offers. Natuzzi Editions, Brentwood Classics, Urban Barn (top-grain), and EQ3 follow. We generally don’t take bonded or faux leather regardless of brand — the resale market for these is too thin.

    A practical note: leather sectionals in dark, neutral colours — cognac, espresso, charcoal, black, slate — carry stronger pre-owned values than bright or pastel hues. Patina and minor wear are usually acceptable; cracking, peeling, or significant staining affects the offer more directly.

    Is my piece a fit?

    Leather sectional configurations we look for

    • Natuzzi leather sectionals (Italia and Editions) — any model in top-grain or higher
    • Pottery Barn leather sectionals in top-grain or higher
    • American Leather sectionals in top-grain or full-aniline
    • Restoration Hardware leather sectionals — Cloud, Lancaster, or Maxwell in particular, though any model qualifies
    • Crate & Barrel leather sectionals in top-grain or higher

    Leather sectionals we don’t typically purchase

    • Bonded leather or split-hide sectionals, including peeling configurations
    • Faux leather, PU-coated, or microfiber upholstery
    • Sectionals labelled simply "genuine leather" (the lowest grade)
    • Significant cracking, peeling, ink marks, or pet damage
    • Mass-market leather sectionals from Ashley, IKEA, Leon's, or The Brick
    • Power-reclining leather sectionals with non-working motors
    • Sleeper sectionals with broken hide-a-bed mechanisms or torn mattress bottoms

    For pieces in those categories, donation through Find Edmonton Furniture Bank, the Bissell Centre, or Habitat for Humanity ReStore is usually the better path. If you’re unsure, send photos — leather grade is often clearer in good photos than the original label suggests.

    Why Edmonton sellers contact us about a leather sectional

    Updating a space or remodeling

    A new piece of furniture may be on the way, an upcoming move, home decor is changing, a colour palet refresh, or a full on renovation/remodel means the old sectional isn't quite a fit anymore.

    Replacing with something smaller or different

    Downsizing the footprint, going to a sofa-and-chair setup, or moving to a fabric piece for daily wear. The leather is still good; the room just calls for something else.

    Lifestyle no longer suits leather

    New kids, pets, or allergy considerations mean a delicate leather piece isn’t practical. The sectional is fine; the household just needs something more forgiving.

    Modular pieces going as a set

    Sectional with matching ottoman, chair, or companion sofa. We can take the qualifying companion pieces in one trip.

    What affects the offer
    • Leather grade: full-aniline and semi-aniline lead; top-grain follows; bonded or split leather is typically outside our scope
    • Brand: premium European, American, and Canadian manufacturers set the offer range
    • Colour: cognac, espresso, charcoal, black, and slate resell fastest; pastel or saturated hues narrow the buyer pool
    • Surface condition: minor wear is fine; cracking, peeling, or significant staining reduces offers
    • Configuration: modular leather sectionals with all original modules outperform partials

    How to photograph a leather sectional for an accurate offer

    Photos let us identify the leather grade, configuration, and condition without an in-home visit. A few specific shots help us quote accurately the first time.

    • Wide shot of the full configuration. Stand back far enough that all modules are in frame — L, U, or straight layout, with the chaise direction visible. This anchors the offer to the actual piece, not a guess.
    • Close-ups of the leather surface. Get within six inches of the seat and back panels. Natural variation (small marks, subtle colour shifts) tells us aniline or semi-aniline; uniform finish tells us top-grain or coated.
    • Seam and edge close-ups. Show how the leather meets at corner seams, headrest tops, and along armrests. These areas show wear, cracking, or peeling first.
    • Underside or back of one module. Where the manufacturer tag usually lives. The tag often spells out the leather grade and model line — both speed up the offer.
    • Any wear, damage, or repaired areas. Don’t hide these — undisclosed surprises at pickup affect the visit. Photograph them honestly and we’ll factor them in transparently.

    A short note about where the piece lives (basement, main floor, walk-up vs. elevator) helps us plan pickup logistics — particularly for U-shape and large modular configurations.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    How do I know what grade of leather my sectional has?

    The original purchase documents or product tag usually specify. If you don't have those, photos of the edges, seams, and surface texture let us identify the grade in most cases.

    My leather has minor cracking on the headrest — does that disqualify it?

    Not necessarily. Localised cracking affects the offer but doesn't automatically rule the piece out. Send photos and we'll give you a straight answer.

    Do I need to arrange anything for the pickup?

    No — we handle disassembly, and load-out. Leather sectionals are heavy and awkward to move without the right equipment; we bring everything needed and take care of it from your living room to the truck.

    Is a faux-leather sectional worth anything to you?

    Almost never. Faux and bonded leather don't hold up over time, and the resale market for either is too thin to support a fair offer.

    Where can I sell my leather sectional in Edmonton?

    Edmonton Refreshed buys leather sectionals directly from sellers across Edmonton, St. Albert, Sherwood Park, Spruce Grove, Leduc, and Fort Saskatchewan — aniline, semi-aniline, and top-grain leather configurations from recognized brands. We handle disassembly and load-out, and pay before we leave. Submit photos through the form on this page and we'll get you an offer today.

    How do I identify what leather grade my sectional is?

    The original purchase documents or hang tag usually specify the grade. If you don't have those, close-up photos of the surface, edges, and seams let us identify it in most cases — aniline and semi-aniline leathers have natural variation and absorb light differently than top-grain or corrected-grain, and the differences are visible in good photos.

    Will I get more for my leather sectional on Marketplace?

    Possibly — quality leather sectionals are one of the stronger Marketplace categories. The honest trade is that with the right buyer, the right configuration, and time to wait, a Marketplace listing can outperform our offer. Our offer trades some price for certainty, leather-grade identification done up front, and pickup logistics handled by people who move leather pieces routinely.

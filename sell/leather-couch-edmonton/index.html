Sell Your Leather Couch in Edmonton

Pre-owned leather couches from quality brands are one of the most consistent categories we buy. Quality leather ages well, retains its structure, and draws steady buyer demand in Edmonton year-round. If you have one from a recognised brand, the form below is the fastest way to find out what we'd pay.

Get an Offer

Leather grade matters more than the word "leather"

A "leather couch" is a wide category. The grade has a meaningful effect on what we can offer. From the top down: full-aniline (undyed, develops genuine patina, no surface coating), semi-aniline (light protective coat, near-top quality), top-grain (the standard at premium brands — durable, slightly more uniform-looking), then corrected-grain and split leather (entry-tier, less resaleable). We focus on top-grain and higher.

The brands that pair good leather with good construction are the ones we actively look for: Natuzzi Italia and Editions, B&B Italia, American Leather, Brentwood Classics, Restoration Hardware, Crate & Barrel leather lines, Urban Barn (top-grain only), EQ3 (Remi line), and Pottery Barn leather configurations selectively.

A practical note: leather couches in neutral, rich colourways — cognac, espresso, charcoal, black, and distressed finishes — resell faster than bright or pastel hues. Minor wear and patina are usually fine. Cracking, peeling, ink marks, and pet damage affect offers more directly — send photos in those cases and we'll give you an honest assessment, but expect a reduced offer.

What affects the offer

  • Leather grade — the word "leather" covers a wide range; full-aniline and semi-aniline command the strongest offers, top-grain is our floor, bonded and faux are generally out
  • Brand — the manufacturer sets the ceiling on what we can offer more than any other single factor
  • Colour — cognac, espresso, charcoal, black, and slate are consistent movers; bold or pastel hues reduce the buyer pool
  • Age and use — 0–6 years in a low-traffic household is the strongest band; well-maintained older pieces still considered if the frame is solid and quiet
  • Distressed finishes — intentionally distressed leather in good structural condition is desirable and prices well

Leather couch types we look for

  • Quality leather couches in any seat count — two, three, or larger
  • Full-aniline leather in any configuration — actively sought
  • Distressed leather couches with solid frames, particularly from Italian and European manufacturers
  • Standalone couches without a matching set — a missing loveseat or chair doesn't affect eligibility

    Frequently Asked Questions

    How can I tell what grade of leather my couch is?

    The original tag or purchase documents usually specify. Without those: high-grade leather feels soft, breathes, and shows natural variation in tone and grain. Lower-grade leathers tend to feel uniform and slightly plasticky with no visible natural variation. Photos help us confirm either way.

    My leather is faded on one cushion — is that a deal-breaker?

    Not a deal-breaker, but it factors into the offer. Send photos and we'll tell you how much it matters.

    Will you take a leather couch with a broken recliner motor?

    Sometimes — a non-functioning mechanism limits the buyer pool and reduces the offer, but a piece that's otherwise in good condition isn't automatically out. Send photos and we'll give you a straight answer.

    My couch is "Italian leather" — does that mean it’s premium?

    Not automatically. Italian leather is a marketing term more than a quality grade. The actual grade — full-aniline, semi-aniline, top-grain — is what determines resale value. Italian-made and Italian-leather aren't the same thing either: the hide may originate in Italy while the sofa was manufactured elsewhere.

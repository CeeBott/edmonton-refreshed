Sofa Trade-In in Edmonton: Sell Your Old Sofa Direct

Updated October 2026

To trade in a sofa in Edmonton, you first need a store that will take one. None of the premium brands we buy runs a trade-in or buy-back program, and IKEA’s sell-back program doesn’t take sofas. Edmonton Refreshed is the direct alternative: send photos for a no-obligation offer, and if you accept, pickup is scheduled with you and you’re paid before we leave.

Many of the pieces we buy come from people replacing a good sofa, not getting rid of a bad one. Below: what each brand actually offers, what to ask if a store does offer a trade-in, and how to time the swap.

    Which furniture brands take trade-ins?

    We checked each brand’s own site in September 2026. None of the brands we buy most often runs a buy-back or trade-in program for used furniture. Several run “trade programs,” but those are discounts for interior designers and other professionals buying new, and the name is where most of the confusion starts.

    • Crate & Barrel: no trade-in or buy-back. Its Trade Program is a discount for interior designers and other professionals.
    • West Elm: no trade-in or buy-back. Its Design Trade Program is for interior designers, and its open-box deals are returned stock.
    • Pottery Barn: no trade-in or buy-back. Same pattern: a Design Trade Program for interior designers, and open-box deals on returns.
    • Restoration Hardware: no trade-in or buy-back. The RH Trade Program is a membership for design, architecture, and build professionals.
    • EQ3: no trade-in or buy-back. EQ3 Trade offers perks to interior designers, architects, and home builders.
    • Rove Concepts: no trade-in or buy-back. Its Trade Program and paid Members program are both discounts on new orders.
    • Natuzzi: no trade-in or buy-back. Its dealer program is for the retailers that sell the brand.
    • American Leather: no trade-in or buy-back. It sells through retailers, and its Trade Program is for trade professionals.
    • IKEA: store credit, but not for sofas. Its Sell-back program takes assembled IKEA pieces like dressers, dining tables, and desks. Sofas and fabric-upholstered armchairs aren’t accepted.

    If you’re buying from an independent Edmonton retailer, it’s worth asking whether that store runs a trade-in promotion of its own. If it does, the questions further down this page will help you weigh it.

    Why upgraders sell to us instead of waiting on a trade-in

    The new sofa has a delivery date

    Once the new piece is booked, the old one needs a way out. Selling it outright means no listing to run while you wait.

    The store won’t take the old one

    None of the premium brands we buy runs a trade-in program, so the outgoing piece is yours to move either way.

    Cash, not store credit

    Store credit only works where it was issued. We pay cash or e-transfer, so the money can go toward the new sofa or anywhere else.

    No listing in move-in week

    Marketplace can pay more if you have time to list, answer messages, and host viewings. If you’d rather settle it in one conversation, that’s what we do.

    Questions to ask if a store offers a trade-in

    Some retailers run their own trade-in promotions. They can be a good deal, but terms vary, so ask a few things before you count on one:

    • Can the credit only be spent there? A trade-in credit is a discount on your next purchase from that store, not money you can take elsewhere.
    • Who moves the old piece? Find out whether the store collects it on delivery day or you have to bring it in.
    • What happens to it afterward? Some trade-in promotions donate the old furniture. That’s good for the community, but it means the credit is a sales incentive rather than a valuation of your sofa.

    If the numbers work, a store trade-in can be the simplest option. If they don’t, a direct offer is one form away.

    Store trade-in, private sale, or selling to us

    The honest version: a private sale usually pays the most if you have the time, and our offer comes in below it. What we offer is pickup handled for you and payment before the piece leaves.

    • Store trade-in, where offered: credit toward a new piece from that store, at a value set by its promotion. The effort depends on the store.
    • Private sale: often the highest price, with time and the right buyer. You handle the listing, messages, viewings, and moving it out.
    • Selling to us: an offer below a private sale, paid by cash or e-transfer before the piece leaves. You send photos, and we handle pickup.

    Timing the swap

    The practical problem with upgrading is overlap: the new sofa has a delivery window, and the old one has nowhere to go. A few things make it easier:

    • Send photos as soon as the new piece is ordered. That leaves time for an offer and a pickup date before delivery week.
    • Tell us the delivery date and any building rules. Condo elevator bookings and loading-dock hours matter for a pickup as much as a delivery.
    • Include matching pieces. If a chair or ottoman goes with the sofa, send photos of it too.

    If the timing is tight, say so when you send photos. We’ll tell you plainly what we can do.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    Can I trade in my old sofa when I buy a new one in Edmonton?

    Usually not at the brands we buy. Crate & Barrel, West Elm, Pottery Barn, RH, EQ3, Rove Concepts, Natuzzi, and American Leather don’t run trade-in or buy-back programs, and IKEA’s sell-back program doesn’t take sofas. Some independent retailers run promotions of their own, so ask the store you’re buying from. Otherwise, selling the old piece outright gets you the same result: the old sofa gone and money back toward the new one.

    Does IKEA buy back sofas?

    No. IKEA Canada’s Sell-back program gives in-store credit for some assembled IKEA furniture, such as dressers, nightstands, dining tables, and desks, but sofas and fabric-upholstered armchairs aren’t accepted. We don’t buy IKEA sofas either, so donation is usually the better route for them.

    What is a furniture trade program?

    It’s a discount or benefits program for interior designers, architects, and other professionals buying new furniture for clients. Crate & Barrel, West Elm, Pottery Barn, RH, EQ3, Rove Concepts, and American Leather all run one. Despite the name, none of them takes used furniture in trade.

    Can you pick up my old sofa when my new one is delivered?

    We can't promise it. Tell us the delivery date when you send photos; if you accept an offer, pickup is scheduled with you.

    Do you pay cash or store credit?

    Cash or e-transfer, paid before the piece leaves your home. There’s no store credit, so the money can go toward a new sofa from any store.

    Do I have to commit to anything when I send photos?

    No. Sending photos starts a conversation. You’re not obligated to accept an offer, and there’s no follow-up pressure if it isn’t the right fit.

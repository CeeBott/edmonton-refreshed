Seller’s Guide

How to Sell Furniture on Facebook Marketplace in Edmonton

Edmonton Refreshed · Collin Bottrell

Selling furniture on Facebook Marketplace goes best when the listing answers buyers’ questions before they ask, the price matches comparable pieces, and viewings and pickup are arranged clearly. This guide walks through each step, from preparing the piece to getting paid.

How long a sale takes varies with the price, condition, size, location, and demand for the piece. There’s no standard timeline, so build in time to adjust if you have a deadline.

Prepare the Piece

  • Clean it. Vacuum the cushions and crevices, lint-roll fabric, and wipe down leather or wood.
  • Gather every part. Cushions, throw pillows, legs, hardware, and any spare covers. A missing piece belongs in the listing.
  • Find the label. Note the brand, model, fabric or leather, and colour. Buyers search by these names.
  • Measure it. Width, depth, and height, and each piece of a sectional separately.
  • Check the way out. Doorways, stairs, and elevator, and whether legs or modules need to come off. Our guide to checking a sofa’s quality covers what to look at while you’re there.

How to Photograph Furniture for Marketplace

  • Take photos in even lighting that shows the colour accurately. Clear the space around the piece.
  • Make the first photo show the whole piece and its full shape. Straight on works well for a sofa; a slight angle often shows a chaise or sectional configuration better.
  • Add both sides, the back, and an angle that shows its depth.
  • Get close-ups of the upholstery or leather so buyers can see the texture and colour.
  • Photograph the label or badge.
  • Show any damage or wear plainly. Buyers will see it at the viewing anyway.
  • For a sectional, show each piece and the full configuration.

Writing the Listing

The title should name the brand, model, and type of piece, and one detail a buyer would search for, such as the fabric or the number of pieces.

The description should cover:

  • Dimensions: overall, and each piece of a sectional.
  • For a sectional, its orientation: which side the chaise or corner is on, as you face it.
  • Material and colour.
  • Age, and where it was bought if you know.
  • Condition in plain words, using grades buyers recognize, including any damage, and whether the home has pets or smokers.
  • Anything missing.
  • Pickup: your area, the floor it’s on, stairs or an elevator and any booking rules, parking, whether it comes apart, and that the buyer needs a suitable vehicle and help.
  • How you’ll be paid.

Example: a complete listing

Here is how a complete listing might look for a Rove Concepts Milo sectional like the one we bought from a home in the Regency Park area of Sherwood Park. The piece details come from our own record of it; the bracketed lines and the price are yours to fill in.

Rove Concepts Milo 6-piece modular sectional, Pearl Chatou bouclé
Six modules: a corner piece, two armless middle pieces, an arm piece, an extra armless module, and an ottoman, plus two throw cushions. Assembled as a corner sectional, 126.5 by 126.5 inches and 31.5 inches high, with a 25.2-inch seat depth; the extra module is 39 by 39.5 inches. Off-white wool-blend bouclé over a kiln-dried hardwood frame with goose-feather-topped foam cushions. Excellent condition, no visible wear. [Pets or smokers in the home: say which, or none.] Modules separate for moving. [Your floor, stairs or elevator, and parking.] You’ll need a truck or large van and a helper. Cash or e-transfer at pickup.

  • Title: brand, model, piece count, and fabric, the terms a buyer would search for.
  • Configuration: every piece listed, so a buyer knows exactly what’s included and can plan the layout.
  • Dimensions: the assembled size and the extra module, so buyers can check their room before messaging.
  • Condition and home: stated plainly, which answers two of the most common questions.
  • Pickup: where it is, how it comes apart, and what vehicle to bring, which filters out buyers who can’t collect it.

A smaller piece follows the same pattern with fewer lines. Our guides to selling a leather sofa and selling a fabric sofa cover how to describe each material.

Pricing It

Price from comparable listings for the same or similar pieces in similar condition, adjusted for the differences, not from what you paid. Brand matters too; our guide to sofa brands with the best resale value covers which names buyers look for. Our guide to what a used sofa is worth walks through the method.

A low offer doesn’t establish what a piece is worth; it’s one buyer’s opening. You can decline, counter, or wait. And a listing that hasn’t sold doesn’t mean you have to accept whatever is offered. Before lowering the price, check the photos, dimensions, description, and pickup details, and whether comparable pieces now offer better value.

Managing Inquiries and Viewings

  • Answer with the key facts. A reply that repeats the dimensions, condition, and pickup details saves a round of questions.
  • Set your own availability. Offer viewing windows that suit you. You don’t have to accommodate every buyer’s schedule.
  • Ask buyers to confirm before they leave home. A quick message on the day saves you waiting for someone who isn’t coming.
  • Be clear what a visit is. Agree whether it’s a viewing to inspect the piece or a committed pickup, in which case the buyer brings the vehicle, help, and payment.
  • State your hold policy. Decide whether you’ll hold a piece, for how long, and on what terms, and say so in writing. A hold with no end time keeps the piece off the market.

Arranging Pickup

Before the pickup day, confirm with the buyer:

  • The vehicle: big enough for the piece, or each module, with the measurements you’ve given.
  • Helpers: how many people they’re bringing to carry it.
  • Stairs, elevator, and parking, and book the elevator if your building requires it.
  • Disassembly: whether legs or modules come off, and who will do it.
  • The time, and how they’ll pay.

On the day, clear the path, protect door frames and floors, and take payment before the piece is loaded.

Selling Large Furniture and Sectionals on Marketplace

Large pieces need the most planning, because the buyer must be able to get them home and into their room. Describe each module and its size, state the orientation, and say whether you’ll sell pieces separately. Note how the sectional comes apart and what vehicle it needs. Buyers who can see that it will fit, and that they can move it, are the ones who follow through.

Getting Paid Safely

Agree on how you’ll be paid before pickup. Cash and Interac e-Transfer are the usual choices; neither removes every risk, so the habits below matter more than the method.

  • Confirm an e-Transfer in your own banking app before the piece leaves, not from a notification email or text. Fake payment notifications are used to steal banking logins, so never log in through a link in one.
  • Make sure you’re receiving money, not sending it. If a payment message asks you to approve or send funds, it’s a money request, not a payment. You should never have to pay a fee or send money to receive payment.
  • Refuse overpayment. If a buyer sends more than the agreed price and asks you to return the difference, don’t. The original payment may not be genuine.
  • Keep codes and passwords private. Don’t share a verification code sent to your phone or any banking details.
  • Consider Autodeposit for e-Transfers, so payments go straight into your account without a separate step.
  • Have someone with you for viewings and pickup, and keep the piece near the door if you can.

If something goes wrong, contact your bank right away and report it to the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre at 1-888-495-8501. Forward suspicious e-Transfer notifications to phishing@interac.ca. Interac’s e-Transfer security page and the Anti-Fraud Centre’s merchandise scams page have more detail.

Is Selling It Yourself Worth It?

Weigh what the piece is likely to sell for against the work: preparing it, photographing it, answering messages, and being home for viewings and pickup. A few hundred dollars can easily be worth that effort, whatever brand the piece is. If it isn’t worth it to you, a free listing, donation, or consignment may suit it better; our guide to who buys used sofas and sectionals in Edmonton covers those options, and our guide to selling furniture before a move covers working to a deadline.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I sell furniture on Facebook Marketplace?

Clean the piece and gather every part, photograph it in even lighting that shows the colour accurately, from every side with close-ups of the upholstery, label, and any damage, and write a listing with the brand, model, dimensions, condition, and pickup details. Price it from comparable listings, agree on viewing times that suit you, confirm the buyer’s vehicle and help before pickup, and take payment before it leaves.

How long does it take to sell furniture on Facebook Marketplace?

There’s no standard timeline. It varies with the price, condition, size, location, and demand for the piece. A clear, well-photographed listing priced against comparables gives it the best chance; if you have a deadline, set a date to review the listing and plan a fallback.

How should I price furniture on Marketplace?

Start from comparable listings for the same or similar pieces in similar condition, adjust for differences such as age, wear, and missing parts, and choose a price that suits your timeline. Our guide to what a used sofa is worth explains the method in detail.

Should I hold furniture for a Marketplace buyer?

That’s your call. Decide whether you’ll hold a piece, for how long, and on what terms, and say so in writing. A hold with no end time keeps the piece off the market while the buyer decides.

What’s the safest way to get paid for furniture on Marketplace?

Agree on payment before pickup and take it before the piece leaves. For an Interac e-Transfer, confirm the money has arrived by checking your own banking app, not a notification. Never log in through a link in a payment message, never send money or pay a fee to receive a payment, refuse overpayments, and don’t share verification codes. If something goes wrong, contact your bank and the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre.

How do I sell a large sectional on Marketplace?

Describe each module with its size, state which side the chaise or corner is on, say how it comes apart and whether you’ll sell pieces separately, and tell buyers what vehicle and help they’ll need. Confirm stairs, elevator booking, and disassembly before the pickup day.

What should I do if a buyer offers much less than my asking price?

A low offer doesn’t establish what the piece is worth; it’s one buyer’s opening. You can decline, counter, or wait for other interest. If the listing hasn’t drawn interest, check the photos, description, pickup details, and comparable prices before lowering your price.

Is there an alternative to selling furniture on Marketplace in Edmonton?

Yes. You can consign it, donate it, or give it away, depending on the piece. If you have a premium sofa or sectional from a recognized brand, roughly ten years old or newer and in good condition, you can submit it to Edmonton Refreshed for consideration. If accepted, our offer includes pickup, with no separate pickup fee.

Have a premium sofa or sectional? Send photos and a few details for an outright offer.

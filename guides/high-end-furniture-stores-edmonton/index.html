High-End Furniture Stores in Edmonton: Who Carries What

Edmonton Refreshed · Collin Bottrell

If you’re shopping for high-end furniture in Edmonton, the hard part usually isn’t the budget. It’s finding the brand. Most of the city’s better stores are independent, and each one carries several makers, so a brand you want might sit on one showroom floor and nowhere else in town. A few well-known brands have no Edmonton store at all.

This guide maps it out. Every store below is described from its own website, checked in October 2026, and we haven’t ranked them. They’re good at different things. Stores change what they carry, so check with the store before you make the trip.

Modern and Contemporary

Scandia Furniture

Scandia calls itself “the foremost destination for modern furniture in Edmonton,” and its tagline sums up the range: “Contemporary European Designs. Lasting Quality. Since 1968.” It’s locally owned and family-operated, with a 13,000 square foot showroom in northwest Edmonton at 13513 156 Street NW. Beyond living rooms, it covers dining, bedroom, entertainment, and home office furniture.

Two brands stand out for sofa shoppers. Scandia’s product pages list Natuzzi Italia, the top line from the Italian leather maker; if you’re comparing it with the more accessible Natuzzi Editions line, our Natuzzi guide explains the difference. Scandia also has its own Stressless section, covering sofas, recliners, and dining, and it’s listed as a dealer on Stressless’s site. There’s a clearance section too, worth a look before you order new.

Website: scandiafurniture.com

F2 Furnishings

F2 describes itself as “the premier destination for Modern and Contemporary style in Alberta.” It opened in Edmonton in 2004 and now serves both Edmonton and Calgary; its Edmonton showroom is at 4219 99 Street NW. Alongside its own independently designed furniture lines, its brands page lists EQ3, Natuzzi Editions, Gus* Modern, ALF Italia, Trica, Dinec, and BDI, among others.

Much of what F2 sells is made to order, so you can choose materials, dimensions, and details. Its FAQ notes lead times from immediate to six to eight months for custom European pieces. If you want to see EQ3 in Edmonton, F2 carries it; our EQ3 guide covers how EQ3 is built and how it holds up.

Two lines in F2’s FAQ are worth knowing if you’re replacing a piece: “Please note that we do not remove and dispose of existing furniture,” and “We do not buy used furniture.” Plan for the old sofa before delivery day.

Website: f2furnishings.ca

LightForm (and Dwell Modern)

LightForm is a Canadian lighting company that has moved into furniture: in its own words, “We’ve built our reputation by lighting Canada’s most inspiring spaces. Now, we’re helping furnish them too.” It has four showrooms across Canada, and Edmonton’s is at 10545 124 Street. If you remember Dwell Modern, its website now redirects to LightForm.

LightForm’s furniture section lists three names any design buyer will know: Cassina, Ligne Roset, and Knoll. Cassina is the Italian maker behind many of the 20th century’s best-known designer chairs and sofas, Ligne Roset is the French company behind the Togo, and Knoll is the American maker of modern classics. If you’re after a recognized design piece rather than a sofa from a general showroom, this is the place to start. The Edmonton showroom keeps weekday hours, so check before you go.

Website: lightformshop.com

Full-Service Design Showrooms

All four of these stores offer design help, including consultations in your home, as part of buying furniture.

McElheran’s Furniture + Design

McElheran’s has been providing “high-end designer furniture” to Edmonton for over 25 years, from 10115 124 Street NW, and describes itself as a family-run local business. It’s the most brand-heavy store on this list: its brands page runs to almost forty names, including Bernhardt, Century Furniture, Hancock & Moore, Theodore Alexander, Thayer Coggin, Sherrill, and American Leather’s Comfort Sleeper sofa beds. The list leans toward established upholstery and case-goods makers, with a few Italian names, like Nicoline Italia and Incanto Italia, in the mix.

Its design team offers complimentary consultations in the store, usually about an hour, and in your home for larger projects, followed by a presentation of recommendations back at the store. If you want help with a whole room rather than a single sofa, start here.

Website: mcelherans.com

Cottswood Interiors

Cottswood began as a small gift shop on 102 Avenue and has been on Mayfield Road, at 10940 Mayfield Road NW, for over 20 years. Its brands page runs to dozens of makers, including American Leather, Lee Industries, CR Laine, Vanguard, Bernhardt, Hickory Chair, and Calligaris. If you’re looking for American Leather in Edmonton, Cottswood and McElheran’s both list it.

Its complimentary design process runs from a showroom visit and an in-home consultation to a concept presentation, final selections, and white-glove delivery. Whichever maker you’re looking at, our how to tell if a sofa is high quality guide covers the questions worth asking in the showroom: frames, cushion fill, and fabric.

Website: cottswood.com

Signature Lane

Signature Lane has been Edmonton owned and operated since 2006, at 10915 170 Street NW, and describes its range as “custom quality furniture” running from classic to contemporary. Its supplier list includes Van Gogh Designs, Four Hands, Hekman, Universal Furniture, Stylus Sofas, and Trica, alongside rugs, art, and accessories. For sofa shoppers, Van Gogh Designs is the name to know, and it’s one of the brands we buy pre-owned.

One thing sets it apart: Signature Lane sells off its floor, so “you get exactly what you see and you don’t have to wait.” Its sales associates are designers, and it offers complimentary home consultations, custom-order upholstery, floor layouts, and custom drapery, plus extended warranties on furniture.

Website: sli-edm.com

CosaFina

CosaFina calls itself “a lifestyle boutique specializing in interiors and fine furnishings,” and has been doing it since 2007, at #102, 10910 105 Avenue. Upholstery and seating make up most of its catalogue, alongside fine linens and decor. Its brands page lists Verellen and Hickory Chair with makers like Michel Ferrand and Oly.

Its interior design team offers in-home consultations, space planning, product and fabric selection, custom furniture options, and re-upholstery. CosaFina keeps shorter hours than the big showrooms, Wednesday to Saturday with other days by appointment, so check its site before you go.

Website: cosafina.ca

Eclectic Home Furnishings

Reside Furnishings

Reside is a family-run store with a West Edmonton showroom at 10434 Mayfield Road. It describes its range as “unique furniture and home decor made from reclaimed and sustainable materials,” mixes styles on purpose (“eclecticism,” in its words), and promises to be “on-trend and on-budget.”

For sofa shoppers, the draw is Stressless: Reside says it has “one of Edmonton’s largest in-stock selections” of Stressless sofas and recliners, along with Stressless dining and office pieces, and it’s listed as a dealer on Stressless’s site. It also sells Canadian-made Handstone dining furniture. If a Stressless is on your list, Reside and Scandia are both dealers, so it’s worth trying models at both.

Website: shopreside.ca

Brand-Owned Stores

Crate & Barrel has a store at Southgate Centre, which makes it one of the few brands in this price range with its own Edmonton showroom. Our Crate & Barrel guide covers which models hold up.

RH has a gallery at Southgate Centre.

Urban Barn has several Edmonton stores, including South Common, Mayfield Common, and Skyview.

Brands With No Edmonton Store

A few names people search for don’t have a store here, and it saves a trip to know it:

  • Pottery Barn lists Calgary’s Chinook Centre as its only Alberta store, and its West Edmonton Mall location no longer appears on its store list. Our Pottery Barn guide covers what to know before buying one sight unseen.
  • West Elm’s only Alberta store is in Calgary, at Mount Royal Village.
  • Rove Concepts has no Edmonton showroom and sells online; our Rove Concepts guide explains what that means for trying one first.

Pre-owned pieces from all three do come up in Edmonton, which is one way to see one in person.

Buying Pre-Owned Instead

Every store above sells new. Edmonton Refreshed sells pre-owned pieces from many of the same makers, including Natuzzi, American Leather, EQ3, Crate & Barrel, Rove Concepts, Pottery Barn, and West Elm, inspected and cleaned before they’re listed.

It’s a different trade-off. Each piece is one of one, so you can’t choose a fabric or a configuration. Replacing a Sofa? Plan for the Old One

New furniture has a delivery date, and the old piece needs somewhere to go. Store policies differ, so ask yours before delivery day. F2, for one, says plainly on its site that it doesn’t remove existing furniture or buy used furniture.

If the outgoing piece is a quality sofa or sectional, it may be worth more than a donation. We buy pieces from Edmonton’s premium stores outright and pick them up, and can often time pickup around your new delivery.

Frequently Asked Questions

Where can I buy Natuzzi in Edmonton?

Scandia Furniture’s product pages list Natuzzi Italia, the brand’s top line, and F2 Furnishings’ brands page lists Natuzzi Editions. Our Natuzzi guide explains the difference between the two lines.

Where can I buy Stressless in Edmonton?

Scandia Furniture and Reside Furnishings both carry Stressless, and both are listed as dealers on Stressless’s own website.

Is there a Pottery Barn or West Elm store in Edmonton?

No. As of October 2026, both brands list Calgary as their only Alberta location: Pottery Barn at Chinook Centre and West Elm at Mount Royal Village. Both sell online, and pre-owned pieces from both turn up in Edmonton.

Will an Edmonton furniture store take away my old sofa?

It depends on the store, so ask before delivery day. F2 Furnishings’ FAQ, for example, says it does not remove or dispose of existing furniture, and that it doesn’t buy used furniture. If the old piece is a quality sofa or sectional, selling it outright is another option.

Are Rove Concepts Sofas Worth It? A First-Hand Look at Build Quality and the Milo

Edmonton Refreshed · Collin Bottrell

Rove Concepts is a Vancouver furniture brand best known for low, wide modular sectionals like the Milo, sold mostly online. With no showroom in Edmonton, most people here meet one on a website or a resale listing, and the questions are the same either way: is it well built, where is it made, and is it worth the price?

We’ve bought and resold two Milo sectionals, and this guide draws on both, alongside what Rove publishes about its construction. It covers who Rove is, where its furniture is made, what the Milo is built from, how pricing and membership work, how it compares with other brands, and what to check on a used one.

Who Is Rove Concepts?

Rove Concepts was founded in 2011; Arthur Lee is its founder and CEO. It began with mid-century modern and Scandinavian designs and has since added Italian contemporary and Japandi styles. The company is based in Vancouver and describes its furniture as designed there.

Rove sells direct to customers, mainly online, and builds its pricing around a paid membership (see below). As of October 2026 it listed two showrooms: downtown Vancouver, at 480 Granville Street, and Central, Hong Kong. There is no Rove showroom or retailer in Edmonton.

The Milo is Rove’s best-known sectional: a flat, low-profile modular design with wider-than-average pieces that combine into anything from a sofa to a large sectional. It’s the line we’ve bought, so it’s the example this guide returns to.

Where Is Rove Concepts Furniture Made?

Rove designs in Vancouver and manufactures in Asia. In a 2024 post on where its furniture is made, Rove says its pieces are crafted in workshops in Vietnam, Malaysia, China, and other countries, with its own teams in each country overseeing production from design to final assembly. Its About page says it handcrafts the majority of its furniture in a workshop it owns and operates.

So Rove is a Canadian company, but its furniture isn’t made in Canada. For a particular piece, the label shows the country of manufacture.

What Rove Concepts Actually Builds

Rove’s product pages list construction details, which is more than many online brands publish. For the Milo, Rove lists:

  • Frame: kiln-dried hardwood with doweled joints and reinforced corner blocks, padded with polyurethane foam wrapped in polyester fibre.
  • Suspension: 8-gauge sinuous springs.
  • Cushions: three layers of high-density foam topped with 100% goose feathers.
  • Legs: round, low-profile solid stainless steel.
  • Testing: tested to ANSI/BIFMA safety and durability standards.
  • Connectors: none. Rove says connectors aren’t included or compatible with the Milo collection.

Our guide to telling whether a sofa is well built explains what each of those means. Of the two Milos we bought, the 2018 one was in good condition and the 2023 one in excellent condition.

Fabrics. Rove offers a wide range of upholstery, and the choices change by product and over time. Rove describes the Chatou bouclé on our 6-piece Milo as tightly looped wool fibres. Before you buy, check the specific fabric’s composition, cleaning instructions, and durability rating on the product page, and order swatches: a fabric on screen and in hand can look quite different.

Warranty and returns. The Milo page lists a five-year Rove limited warranty and 30-day returns. If you’re buying used, ask Rove whether the warranty transfers before you count on it.

The Milo: The Brand’s Defining Piece

The design is architectural: low, with flat surfaces, slightly curved arms, and seats that reward lounging rather than sitting upright. Rove lists the preset four-piece at 133.4 by 78.7 inches and 31.5 inches high, with a 25.2-inch seat depth and a 17.7-inch seat height. That depth suits people who want to stretch out and is worth measuring against a smaller room before you commit.

The modular system is the Milo’s most practical feature. Corner pieces, armless middles, arm pieces, and ottomans combine into configurations of almost any size and can be rearranged later as a room or household changes.

One practical note: the pieces don’t lock together. They sit side by side and stay in place through weight and positioning, and Rove says connectors aren’t compatible with the collection. That’s common with modular sectionals, but worth knowing if you have kids, pets, or a smooth floor.

We’ve carried the Milo 6-piece modular sectional in Pearl Chatou bouclé, and handling it in person confirmed what the photos only suggest: the proportions are substantial, it fills a room, and the build matched what the specifications describe.

How Rove Concepts Compares to the Brands You’re Cross-Shopping

Rove Concepts vs. Article and West Elm

These are the brands most often shopped alongside Rove: modern designs sold mainly online. The fairest way to compare them is on what each publishes for the specific piece: frame material and joinery, suspension, cushion layers, whether modules connect, the warranty, and the return policy. Rove publishes all of those for the Milo. Where another brand’s page leaves one out, ask before you buy.

Rove Concepts vs. EQ3

EQ3 is a Winnipeg company that builds its custom upholstery in Canada. Its sofas are sold in Edmonton through F2 Furnishings, and its nearest showroom is in Calgary, so you can sit on one before you buy. Rove has no such option here. Both offer modular designs; check whether a given EQ3 collection’s pieces connect, since the Milo’s don’t.

Rove Concepts vs. Crate & Barrel

Crate & Barrel has an Edmonton store at Southgate, and builds collections such as the Gather and Lounge in North Carolina on kiln-dried hardwood frames. The seats differ: the Gather we bought had innerspring seat cushions, where the Milo uses layered foam topped with goose feathers. Crate & Barrel’s range also covers more styles, while Rove’s leans consistently modern.

Where to Buy Rove Concepts in Edmonton

There is no Rove Concepts showroom in Edmonton, and no local retailer carries the brand. New pieces are sold direct through roveconcepts.com and shipped to you, which means ordering a sofa you haven’t sat on, with lead times and delivery as part of the deal. Rove’s nearest showroom is in Vancouver.

That’s why the used market matters for this brand here: buying locally is the one way to see and sit on a Rove piece before you pay for it.

Buying Pre-Owned Rove Concepts in Edmonton: What to Watch For

The checks below build on the general process for buying a used sofa in Edmonton.

Frame and cushions

Sit in each seat and press firmly, comparing them. A kiln-dried hardwood frame shouldn’t flex noticeably or creak under normal weight; if it does, ask about the piece’s history. If a seat cushion feels uneven, sinks more than the others, or stays flat after fluffing, inspect it more closely and check how it supports you when seated. Fluffing alone won’t tell you whether the problem is compressed filling or worn foam.

Upholstery

Rove’s fabric options age differently. Bouclé varies the most: it can hold up for years in a careful home or pill on the arms and seat edges with heavy use. Velvet shows crushed pile and needs its pile brushed in one direction. Inspect the arms and seat edges in good light. On leather, look for cracking along the seat folds and arms, and follow Rove’s care instructions for that leather rather than a general treatment.

Modular completeness

On a multi-piece sectional, confirm every piece is there, that they sit flush, and that the upholstery matches across them. Rove’s fabric lineup changes: the Pearl Chatou bouclé on our 6-piece Milo wasn’t among the Milo’s preset fabrics in October 2026, so a buyer hoping to add a matching piece later may not find one.

Household history

Ask about pets, children, smoke, and sun. None of them rules a piece out, but each tells you what it has been through. For a broader inspection that applies to any brand, see our guide to furniture condition grades.

Is Rove Concepts Good Quality?

On the Milo, the published construction is solid: a kiln-dried hardwood frame with doweled joints and corner blocks, sinuous springs, and layered high-density foam, tested to ANSI/BIFMA standards. The two Milos we’ve bought held up: the 2018 one in good condition, the 2023 one in excellent condition. The trade-offs are practical rather than structural: no showroom in Edmonton, pricing built around a paid membership, modules that don’t connect, and fabrics that come and go. Judge a specific piece by its frame, cushions, and upholstery, in person where you can.

If you’re weighing it against higher-end leather, our Natuzzi and B&B Italia guides cover those brands.

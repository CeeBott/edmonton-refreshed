What Is a Used Sofa Worth in Edmonton? How to Price Your Couch for Resale

Edmonton Refreshed · Collin Bottrell

To work out what a used sofa is worth, identify exactly what it is, assess its condition honestly, find comparable pieces for sale, adjust for the differences, and choose an asking price that suits how soon it needs to go. The steps below walk through each, then cover what to change if a listing isn’t working.

The same method works for a couch, a loveseat, or a sectional, from any brand.

How to Price a Used Sofa: The Method

1. Identify the piece

Find the label under the cushions or underneath and note the brand, model, fabric or leather, and colour. Measure the width, depth, and height, and for a sectional, each piece and which side the chaise is on. Note the year it was bought, and find the original receipt if you have it. Buyers search by brand and model, and the details let you match it against comparable listings. Our guide to telling if a sofa is high quality covers what the label and specification can tell you.

2. Assess its condition

Look in daylight at the seat fronts, arm tops, and cushions; sit on every seat; check for stains, fading, pilling, and any pet or smoke odour; and make sure every piece, cushion, and mechanism is there and works. Then place it on a scale buyers recognize. Our guide to what condition grades mean sets out the four grades we use, and our guides to leather and fabric sofas cover what to check for each.

3. Find comparable pieces

See the next section. Comparables do more to set a realistic price than anything else.

4. Adjust for the differences

No comparable will match exactly, so adjust up or down for what differs: condition, age, a missing cushion or leg, a recliner that doesn’t work, a less common colour, or a size or configuration that suits fewer rooms. Brand matters too; our guide to sofa brands with the best resale value covers which names buyers look for.

5. Choose an asking price for your timeline

With time to spare, you can start toward the upper end of what comparable pieces in similar condition are listed for and adjust later. With a deadline, start nearer the lower end so the first serious buyers act. If you expect offers, leave some room between your asking price and the lowest price you’ll accept.

How to Choose Useful Comparables

Search Facebook Marketplace and Kijiji, and the brand’s own site for what the same model costs new. Rank what you find by how closely it matches, in roughly this order:

  1. The same model and configuration.
  2. The same kind of upholstery: leather or fabric, and a similar grade.
  3. A similar size.
  4. A similar age.
  5. A similar condition.
  6. For sale in or near Edmonton, where buyers would actually collect it.

Two cautions. An asking price shows you the competition, not what anything sold for. And a listing that disappears doesn’t tell you its final price, or even that it sold. If the same piece keeps reappearing, or sits for weeks, its price is a ceiling rather than a guide.

Three Numbers That Get Confused

  • Your asking price is where you open a private listing. It sets the conversation.
  • The likely sale price is where a private sale lands after any negotiation. It’s the number your asking price should be built around.
  • A reseller’s offer is what a business pays to buy the piece outright. It accounts for the reseller’s costs of pickup, preparation, and resale, and its margin, so it’s usually lower than what a private sale might bring. It reflects what that business can pay, not the definitive value of the furniture.

If Your Listing Isn’t Getting Interest

Before you lower the price, check what else a buyer might be reacting to:

  • Photos. Are they in daylight, with the whole piece, both sides, and close-ups of any wear?
  • Dimensions. Are the width, depth, and height in the listing, and each piece of a sectional?
  • Description. Does it name the brand, model, age, and condition plainly, so buyers who search by name find it?
  • Pickup. Does it say where the piece is in the house, whether there are stairs, and that the buyer needs a vehicle and help?
  • Comparables. Has a similar piece in similar condition been listed for less since you started?

If all of that holds up, lower the price. Pick a date to review the listing based on when the piece has to be gone, for example halfway to a moving date, rather than waiting a set number of weeks. Frequently Asked Questions

How much is a used couch worth?

It depends on the brand and model, its condition and age, its size and configuration, and what comparable pieces are listed for nearby. Identify the piece, grade its condition honestly, compare it with the closest matches on Marketplace and Kijiji, and adjust for the differences. Remember that asking prices show the competition, not what pieces actually sold for.

How much should I sell my used couch for?

Start from comparable listings for the same or similar model in similar condition, adjust for differences such as age, wear, and missing parts, and then choose a price for your timeline: nearer the upper end if you have time, nearer the lower end if you have a deadline. Leave some room if you expect offers.

Is asking half of what I paid for a sofa reasonable?

It depends on the piece. A nearly new sofa from a recognized brand in excellent condition can be priced closer to retail; an older or worn one, or one from a mass-market store, much less. Price from comparable listings and condition rather than from the receipt.

How can I find out what similar sofas sold for?

Marketplace and Kijiji show asking prices, not final sale prices, and a listing that disappears may not have sold at all. Treat asking prices as the competition. A piece that keeps being relisted, or sits for weeks, is priced above what buyers are paying.

Why is a reseller’s offer lower than what I could ask privately?

A reseller pays for pickup, cleaning and preparation, listing, and resale, and needs a margin, so its offer is usually below a private sale price. In return, it handles the selling and pays in full when it collects. The offer reflects what that business can pay, not the definitive value of the furniture.

What should I change if my sofa isn’t selling?

Check the listing before cutting the price: daylight photos of the whole piece and any wear, full dimensions, the brand, model, age, and condition, clear pickup details, and whether a similar piece has since been listed for less. If those are right, lower the price, and set your next review date based on when the piece has to go.

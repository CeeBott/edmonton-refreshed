Who Buys Used Couches and Sofas in Edmonton? Your Options, Compared

By Collin Bottrell · Edmonton Refreshed

You’ve decided to sell. The question is who to sell to — and what you’re actually trading off with each option. There are four realistic channels in Edmonton. Here’s how they compare on the variables that matter.

Facebook Marketplace and Kijiji

Self-listing is where most people start. You post the piece yourself, set your price, and deal with buyers directly. The upside is that this channel offers the highest potential payout — no middleman taking a percentage, no below-market offer from a buyer who needs room to resell. A well-priced, recognized-brand sofa in excellent condition can sell within a week.

The honest version of what this looks like in practice: Edmonton’s Facebook Marketplace is active, but the used furniture category is also saturated. At any given time there are hundreds of sofas listed, which gives buyers leverage and makes pricing discipline essential. Most pieces take 3–8 weeks to sell. During that window you’re managing a stream of inquiries — a portion of which are lowball offers, a portion of which are people who go quiet after asking “is this still available?” The serious buyers who do follow through need to arrange their own pickup, and for large sectionals, that step breaks down regularly.

If the piece doesn’t sell, you reduce the price and try again. There’s no floor and no guaranteed outcome.

For a detailed look at what this process actually involves, see Selling Furniture on Facebook Marketplace in Edmonton: What They Don’t Tell You.

Furniture Consignment

Consignment stores list and sell the piece on your behalf, then pay you a percentage of the sale price once it closes. You drop the piece off and let them handle it — lower effort than a self-listing, and your piece gets exposure in a physical retail environment rather than competing with everything else on Marketplace.

The main limitation is time. Consignment pieces routinely take 4–12 weeks to sell, and there’s no guaranteed date. You also don’t control the listing price or how the piece is presented. Your return is the sale price minus the store’s commission — typically 40–60% going to you, depending on the store and the agreement.

Intake is selective. Consignment stores in Edmonton won’t take everything — they need pieces they can actually move, which generally means recognized brands in solid condition and neutral colourways. A worn or generic piece is unlikely to be accepted. If it doesn’t sell during the consignment period, you’ll need to arrange pickup or the price gets reduced further.

The Edmonton furniture consignment and resale guide covers how this channel works in more detail, including what you’ll actually net after fees.

Donation

Habitat for Humanity ReStore offers scheduled furniture pickup in Edmonton. Freecycle and Facebook’s free listings also move pieces quickly. If your sofa is in reasonable shape but not at a level you’d realistically price it above a few hundred dollars, donation is often the fastest resolution.

Payout is zero. What you get instead: the transaction is simple, the pickup is scheduled, and the piece is gone without negotiation or no-shows. For furniture that’s past its prime — significant wear, unknown brand, pet damage, foam that’s past recovering — this is often the most realistic option and the one that closes fastest.

Donation isn’t giving up — it’s accurate pricing for what the piece is worth in its current state.

Direct Buyer

A direct buyer purchases the piece outright — not on consignment, not contingent on finding a retail customer. You send photos, receive a firm offer, and if you accept, pickup is handled within days. No listings, no viewings, no negotiating with strangers.

The tradeoff is straightforward: a direct buyer pays less than a successful private sale. They need room to price the piece for resale, which means the offer reflects that margin. What you’re exchanging for that discount is certainty and zero effort — a firm number, logistics handled, transaction closed.

Direct buyers are also selective. Edmonton Refreshed, for example, focuses on recognized-brand sofas and sectionals in good condition — Natuzzi, B&B Italia, Rove Concepts, Crate & Barrel, EQ3, West Elm, American Leather, and similar. A generic or heavily worn piece won’t qualify. If yours does, this is often the cleanest path to a completed transaction.

Which Option Fits Your Situation

If your piece is from a recognized brand and in excellent condition, you have real options. Marketplace can get you the most money if you have the time and tolerance for the process. A direct buyer gets it done in days at a lower price. Consignment sits in the middle on both.

If your piece is in average condition or from a brand buyers don’t recognize, Marketplace is likely your path — priced to reflect that reality. Donation is the right call if condition is genuinely poor or if you need the piece gone without any friction.

Frequently Asked Questions

Where can I sell my used couch in Edmonton?

Your four main options are: Facebook Marketplace or Kijiji (self-listing), furniture consignment stores, donation organizations like Habitat for Humanity ReStore, and direct buyers who purchase pre-owned furniture outright. Each involves a different tradeoff between payout, speed, and effort. The right choice depends on your piece’s brand and condition, your timeline, and how much work you’re willing to put in.

Will furniture consignment shops take any sofa?

No. Consignment stores in Edmonton are selective — they typically require pieces to be in excellent condition, from recognizable brands, and in styles that sell in their market. A generic or heavily worn sofa is unlikely to be accepted. If your piece doesn’t qualify for consignment, Marketplace, donation, or a direct buyer are the realistic alternatives.

How long does it take to sell a used sofa on Facebook Marketplace?

Most used sofas on Edmonton’s Facebook Marketplace take 3–8 weeks to sell. Well-priced pieces from recognized brands in excellent condition can move faster — sometimes within a week. Overpriced pieces or those from unknown brands can sit for months or not sell at all. Edmonton’s Marketplace is active but saturated with used furniture, so pricing relative to what’s actually moving matters more than what you paid.

Do Edmonton furniture buyers come to your home for pickup?

Direct buyers like Edmonton Refreshed handle all pickup — truck and labour included. You don’t need to move the piece, disassemble it, or arrange help. For Marketplace and Kijiji sales, pickup is the buyer’s responsibility, which frequently creates problems with large sectionals when buyers arrive without adequate transport.

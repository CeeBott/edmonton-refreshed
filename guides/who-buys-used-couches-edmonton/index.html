Who Buys Used Couches and Sofas in Edmonton? Your Complete Selling Guide

Edmonton Refreshed · Collin Bottrell

In Edmonton, used couches and sofas are bought by private buyers on Facebook Marketplace and Kijiji, and by a few selective buyers that pay outright for specific kinds of furniture, such as mid-century pieces or premium sofas. Consignment stores don’t buy the couch; they sell it on your behalf and pay you a share once it sells. Charities take donations too, which clears the couch but pays nothing.

Which of these fits depends on what you have, what condition it’s in, and how much time you have. That applies whether you’re clearing one room, downsizing to a smaller home, or settling a family estate.

Who Buys Used Furniture in Edmonton

Four kinds of people and companies take second-hand furniture in Edmonton, and three of them pay for it.

Private buyers. Facebook Marketplace and Kijiji reach the most local buyers, who look for a broad range of furniture. You handle the listing, messages, and viewings. The buyer usually brings a vehicle and pays cash or e-transfer at pickup.

Consignment stores. A consignment store sells the piece on its floor and pays you a share of the final price after it sells. Stores choose what they take, usually by brand, condition, and size, and some expect you to deliver it. Ask about the commission, markdowns, how long the piece stays, and what happens if it doesn’t sell. Our consignment and resale guide covers what to check in an agreement and works through an example payout.

Selective buyers that pay outright. Some companies buy specific categories and pay in full when they collect, such as mid-century dealers and premium sofa resellers like us. Don’t assume a used-furniture store buys from the public; many only sell. Check the business’s own page for what it buys before you send photos.

Charities. Donation is a destination, not a sale. Edmonton charities accept furniture by type and condition, and some pick up.

Which Option Fits Your Furniture

Everyday household furniture

A sofa from IKEA, Ashley, The Brick, Leon’s, or a similar store sells best on Marketplace or Kijiji, priced for its condition. Consignment stores and outright buyers rarely take these pieces. IKEA Canada’s Sell-back program doesn’t accept sofas. If the couch is clean and undamaged but not worth the time to sell, donate it.

Premium sofas and sectionals

A sofa from a recognized brand such as B&B Italia, Natuzzi, Rove Concepts, Crate & Barrel, EQ3, or West Elm has more routes. Buyers on Marketplace search for these names, consignment stores are more likely to accept them, and some resellers buy them outright. A documented brand and model, and the original receipt if you have it, make each route easier. Our guides to selling a leather sofa and selling a fabric sofa cover how to assess one.

Vintage and mid-century furniture

Specialist vintage dealers buy mid-century and older furniture outright, and some collect from Edmonton homes. Look for a dealer’s buying page that lists the eras, makers, and pieces it wants, then send a photo of each piece with its condition, the maker if you know it, and your location. Pieces from known makers in good original condition draw the most interest; a private listing that names the maker and era is the alternative.

Furniture with substantial wear

Charities generally won’t take stained, damaged, or smoke-exposed furniture, and buyers will price heavy wear steeply. A free listing can still find someone who wants it, as long as you give it a deadline. Otherwise, City of Edmonton Eco Stations accept furniture drop-offs (check the accepted items list for fees), the city’s seasonal Big Bin Events take old couches free of charge, and junk removal services collect for a fee.

Where to Donate a Couch in Edmonton

  • Habitat for Humanity ReStore accepts clean, complete, undamaged furniture from pet-free and smoke-free homes, but not sleeper sofas. It offers free pickup in Edmonton, Beaumont, Fort Saskatchewan, Leduc, Nisku, St. Albert, Sherwood Park, and Spruce Grove, but its crews can’t enter homes or move items from basements, so have the couch at the door. Photos with your request help.
  • Find, Homeward Trust’s furniture bank, accepts couches, sofas, and loveseats free of major stains, rips, damage, or strong odours, but not recliner couches, hide-a-beds, or pieces over 6 feet. It offers free scheduled pickup in Edmonton and surrounding areas, with pickups outside Edmonton on weekends. Send photos, a short description, your address, and access details, and expect its drivers to decline items that don’t meet its criteria. You can also drop off at its donation centre at 5120 122 Street NW.
  • Goodwill no longer takes furniture at its stores. Its Impact Centres accept furniture under 50 pounds, but upholstered furniture is on its excluded list, so it isn’t an option for a sofa.

Every charity can decline a piece on capacity or condition, so book before you count on a donation.

How to Sell a Used Couch Yourself

  • Identify it. Find the label under the cushions or underneath and note the brand, model, and fabric or leather. Buyers search by those names.
  • Assess its condition honestly. Check the seat fronts and arms in daylight for wear, stains, and fading, sit on every seat, and note any pet or smoke exposure. Our guide to furniture condition grades explains the terms buyers use.
  • Compare prices. Look for the same model, or similar pieces, in similar condition, and price from there rather than from what you paid. Our guide to what a used sofa is worth covers pricing.
  • Photograph it. Shoot in daylight: the front, both sides, the back, close-ups of any wear, and the label.
  • Describe it. Give the dimensions (each piece of a sectional separately), the age, and the condition in plain words.
  • Arrange the pickup. Say where the couch is in the house, whether there are stairs or an elevator, that the buyer needs a vehicle and help, and how you’ll be paid. Take payment before it leaves.

Selling a Premium Sofa or Sectional to Edmonton Refreshed

We buy premium sofas and sectionals outright. We consider pieces that are:

  • A sofa or sectional (exceptional chairs considered)
  • From a recognized brand or premium retailer that cost $3,000 or more new
  • Roughly ten years old or newer (older premium and designer pieces considered on condition)
  • In good condition, without major damage, staining, or pet damage

Send photos of the front, sides, back, and label, along with the brand and model, roughly when it was bought, the original price if you know it, its condition, and your location and any deadline. We aim to reply the same day. If the piece is a fit, we make an offer based on brand, age, and condition and explain how we got to it. Pickup is included in the offer, with no separate fee. Once you accept, we schedule pickup with you, our crew collects it in an enclosed truck, and you’re paid by cash or e-transfer before it leaves your home.

An outright sale means no listing to manage, no wait for a consignment sale, and payment in full on the day it’s collected. It trades some of what a private sale might bring for that convenience, which can suit a sale on a deadline or a seller looking for an alternative to consignment. Frequently Asked Questions

Who buys used couches in Edmonton?

Private buyers on Facebook Marketplace and Kijiji buy a broad range of furniture. Some businesses buy specific kinds of furniture outright, such as mid-century dealers and premium sofa resellers. Consignment stores don’t buy the piece; they sell selected pieces on your behalf and pay a share after they sell. Charities such as Habitat for Humanity’s ReStore and Find accept donations, which pay nothing.

Where can I sell used furniture for cash in Edmonton?

Private buyers on Marketplace and Kijiji usually pay cash or e-transfer when they collect. Businesses that buy outright, such as vintage dealers and premium sofa resellers, pay in full on pickup for the pieces they accept. Consignment pays only after the piece sells, by the method in the store’s agreement.

Will consignment stores in Edmonton take my sofa?

Only if it suits the store. Stores choose by brand, condition, and size, and some don’t take large upholstered pieces. Before you consign, ask who delivers it, the commission, the markdown schedule, how long it stays, and when you can withdraw it.

Where can I donate a couch in Edmonton?

Habitat for Humanity’s ReStore accepts clean, undamaged furniture from pet-free and smoke-free homes (not sleeper sofas) and offers free pickup in Edmonton, St. Albert, Sherwood Park, Spruce Grove, Leduc, Fort Saskatchewan, Beaumont, and Nisku, though crews can’t enter homes. Find, Homeward Trust’s furniture bank, accepts couches and loveseats (not recliner couches, hide-a-beds, or pieces over 6 feet) and offers free scheduled pickup in Edmonton and surrounding areas, with pickups outside Edmonton on weekends. Book before you rely on either.

Who buys vintage or mid-century furniture in Edmonton?

Specialist vintage dealers buy mid-century and older furniture outright, and some collect from Edmonton homes. Check a dealer’s buying page for the eras and makers it wants, then send photos, the maker if known, the condition, and your location. A private listing that names the maker and era is the alternative.

What can I do with a couch nobody will buy or take?

Try a free listing with a deadline for pickup. If nobody collects it, City of Edmonton Eco Stations accept furniture drop-offs (fees vary by item), the city’s seasonal Big Bin Events take old couches free of charge, and junk removal services collect for a fee.

Can I trade in my old sofa when I buy a new one in Edmonton?

Usually not. When we checked in September 2026, none of the major brands sold in Edmonton that we buy, including Natuzzi, EQ3, Rove Concepts, Crate & Barrel, West Elm, Pottery Barn, Restoration Hardware, and B&B Italia, ran a trade-in or buy-back program, and IKEA Canada’s Sell-back program doesn’t take sofas. Selling the old piece yourself, or to a buyer, gets you money toward the new one instead.

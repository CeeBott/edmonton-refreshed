Selling a Fabric or Bouclé Sofa in Edmonton: What the Resale Market Actually Values

Edmonton Refreshed · Collin Bottrell

What a used fabric sofa sells for in Edmonton depends on its brand and model, its overall condition, its size and configuration, and how its fabric has worn. Whether the label says polyester, performance, or bouclé matters less than the fabric’s condition. Check it closely, describe and photograph what you find, and price it for what a buyer will see.

If you’d rather have us look at it, send photos for a direct offer.

Polyester, Bouclé, and Performance Fabric: What the Label Means

Three words come up on almost every fabric sofa. They answer different questions, and one fabric can be all three.

  • Polyester (or olefin, cotton, linen, wool) is the fibre the fabric is made from.
  • Bouclé is a looped yarn that gives the fabric its nubbly texture. It can be made from polyester, wool, cotton, or a blend.
  • Performance is the maker’s claim that the fabric resists stains or cleans easily. There’s no single standard for it, so read what the maker actually says.

Crate & Barrel’s Thrive fabric on the Aris sectional we sold is a performance fabric, 97% olefin, woven with a bouclé yarn. The Tribute fabric on the Gather we have now is a performance twill made of 100% polyester. None of these words tells you a fabric’s quality by itself. How it feels, how it has worn, and how it cleans do.

Find the cleaning code and care instructions before you clean anything. The code is on the label or the original order: W means water-based cleaners, S means solvent-based only, WS means either, and X means vacuum only. The wrong cleaner can leave a ring or change the colour.

How to Check a Fabric or Bouclé Sofa for Wear

Look in daylight, up close and from a few feet back. Start with the seat fronts, cushion tops, arm tops, and inside arms, where wear shows first. On a sectional, check every module.

Pilling. Small balls of the fabric’s own fibres, where it gets rubbed. A fabric shaver removes them. Our Gather showed pilling after four years of use; we shaved the seat, arms, back, and cushions, then extraction-cleaned it. Test a shaver on a hidden spot first, and don’t use one on bouclé loops or velvet.

Loose fibres in bouclé. Lint, hair, and threads from clothing and blankets get caught in the loops, often in a different colour from the fabric. A lint roller or soft brush lifts most of it. It isn’t wear.

Snags and pulled loops. A loop or thread pulled up out of the weave. Don’t cut or pull it, which can unravel more. Push it back with a blunt needle, or leave it and mention it.

Stains. Clean them by the cleaning code, or have them professionally cleaned. Spot-cleaning can leave its own mark: the B&B Italia Charles we have now shows subtle tonal variation on the seat from earlier spot-cleaning.

Fading. Compare the seat and arms with the back of the sofa or the underside of a cushion. Sun-fading doesn’t clean out. Our Crate & Barrel Rochelle set was clean and solid but significantly faded, and we graded it Fair.

Odour. Smoke and pet odour get into the cushion fill and foam, so cleaning the fabric may not remove them. Ask someone who doesn’t live with the sofa.

Cushions. Look for flat seat cushions, foam that stays dented, or one seat lower than the others. Feather and down cushions often recover with fluffing; the down cushions on the Fabbrica sofa we bought came back like new after eight years. Foam that has broken down doesn’t. If a seat is still low with the cushion off, the problem is in the support underneath: the springs, the webbing, or the frame. Have it looked at before you price it.

Checking a Velvet Sofa

Velvet has a short, dense pile, and the fibres lean one way. Where they’re pressed flat or brushed the other way, they catch the light differently and look lighter or darker. That shading, or crushed pile, can look like a stain when it isn’t one.

To check a mark, brush the pile gently with your hand in each direction and look from a few angles. If the mark changes or fades, it’s probably pile shading, though there can still be a stain underneath. A mark that looks the same from every angle is more likely a stain. Bare or shiny patches where the pile has worn away are permanent wear.

Crushed pile can sometimes be lifted by brushing. Before you steam or clean velvet, follow the manufacturer’s care instructions for that fabric. Some velvets shouldn’t be steamed, and the cleaning code alone doesn’t tell you whether yours can.

Before you photograph velvet, brush each cushion in the same direction so the shading reads evenly.

How to Describe and Price a Used Fabric Sofa

A specific description answers buyers’ questions before they ask. Include:

  • The brand and model, and the fabric name and colour from the label or order (for example, “Tribute in Gravel”).
  • The fibre content and cleaning code.
  • The age and overall dimensions.
  • Whether there are pets or smokers in the home.
  • What you found when you checked it, in plain words: “light pilling on the seat fronts, no stains or fading”.

To estimate its resale value, search Marketplace for the same model, or similar pieces from the same brand, in similar condition. A piece that keeps getting relisted is usually priced above what buyers will pay. Base the price on what a used piece in your condition is worth, not on what you paid. A neutral colour suits more rooms; a bold one can sell well but may take longer to find its buyer. Our guide to what a used sofa is worth covers pricing in more detail.

Photographing a Fabric Sofa

  • Shoot in daylight with overhead lights off, so the colour is true.
  • Remove throws and pillows that aren’t part of the set, and lint-roll the fabric.
  • Take the front straight on, both sides, and the back.
  • Get close-ups of the seat fronts and arms, and of any pilling, snags, stains, or fading.
  • For bouclé, take one close-up of the texture and one from a few feet back.
  • Photograph the label and the cleaning code.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is bouclé furniture hard to sell?

Not if it’s in good condition. Buyers look closely at bouclé for pulled loops, matted areas on the seats, and fibres caught in the loops. Lint and loose fibres lift out with a lint roller; pulled loops and matting are wear. Photograph the texture close up and describe its condition specifically.

Is polyester upholstery bad for resale value?

Not on its own. Polyester is the fibre, and it’s used in everything from budget sofas to performance fabrics like Crate & Barrel’s Tribute on the Gather. Buyers care about how the fabric looks and feels now: pilling, stains, fading, and cushion wear.

What does performance fabric mean on a sofa?

It’s the maker’s claim that the fabric resists stains or cleans easily. There’s no single standard, so check what the maker says about that fabric and its cleaning code. A performance fabric can be polyester, olefin, or another fibre, and can be woven with a bouclé yarn.

How can I tell if a mark on a velvet sofa is a stain?

Brush the pile gently with your hand in each direction and look from a few angles. If the mark changes or fades, it’s probably pile shading, though there can still be a stain underneath. A mark that looks the same from every angle is more likely a stain. Before steaming or cleaning, follow the manufacturer’s care instructions for that fabric; some velvets shouldn’t be steamed.

Can pilling be removed from a sofa?

Usually, with a fabric shaver. Test it on a hidden spot first, and don’t use one on bouclé loops or velvet. Pilling can come back with use, so describe it in your listing even after you’ve removed it.

Does fabric colour affect resale value?

It affects how many buyers a piece suits. Neutral colours fit more rooms; a bold colour can sell well but may take longer to find its buyer. Fading matters more than the colour itself: compare the seat and arms with the back or the underside of a cushion before you list.

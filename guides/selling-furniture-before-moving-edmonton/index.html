Selling Furniture Before a Move in Edmonton: A Timeline Guide

By Collin Bottrell · Edmonton Refreshed

The Real Problem Isn’t What to Sell — It’s Whether You’ll Actually Get It Done in Time

You’ve decided to move. You’ve walked through the house, flagged what won’t fit the new space or isn’t worth paying to move. That part’s done.

Now you have four weeks, or six, or eight — and a sectional, a sofa, maybe a dining set sitting in your living room waiting to become someone else’s problem.

Most people think selling furniture before a move is straightforward. It isn’t. The movers show up — and the piece you planned to sell is still sitting in your living room. Now it either comes with you, adding cost and hassle and the real possibility it doesn’t fit, or you give it away in the final 48 hours for nothing.

That sequence is so common it might as well be a script. This guide exists to help you avoid it.

Why Marketplace Is Harder Than It Looks

Listing furniture takes more time to do properly than most sellers expect. Photos that actually show the piece. A description worth reading. A price low enough to get messages and high enough to be worth the effort. Then the messages arrive — half of them won’t respond to a follow-up, a quarter will negotiate and disappear, and the remaining few will schedule a viewing and maybe show up.

The ones who show up need to arrange their own truck. That coordination alone adds days. Then you need to be home. Then they come back with a friend who can help lift it. Then something comes up — which means restarting the process days later while your move date gets closer.

Edmonton’s moving season makes this harder. June and July flood the market. Your piece is competing with every other person in your neighbourhood doing the same thing on the same timeline. Well-priced, brand-name pieces do move quickly in that environment. Everything else waits — and waiting has a cost when you have a move date.

Winter is its own problem. Between October and March, buyers are less motivated to haul furniture across town in −20°C weather. Listings that would move in two weeks in July regularly take six weeks in February.

The timeline you think you have is shorter than it feels.

The Real Cost of Moving a Piece You Could Sell

Here’s the math most sellers don’t run explicitly: moving furniture costs money, and the cost is higher than people expect. A professional two-person crew in Edmonton charges by the hour. A large sectional gets disassembled, wrapped, loaded, transported, unloaded, reassembled — call it one and a half to three hours depending on distance and difficulty. That’s real money, spent on moving a piece that may not fit the new space the way you’re imagining it will.

When you run the comparison — what you’d net selling the piece versus what it actually costs to move it, plus the risk it doesn’t work in the new room — the gap is often much smaller than it looks. Selling gets it gone. Clean. No moving cost for that piece, no uncertainty, no sectional sitting wrong in the new living room for six months until you sell it anyway.

The question worth asking isn’t what is this worth at retail. It’s: what does it actually cost me — in time, money, and stress — to get what it’s worth?

The Channel You Choose Depends on the Time You Have

Marketplace can work — but under a fixed move timeline, it becomes a risk most sellers underestimate. Different channels have different time requirements, and treating every piece as a Marketplace candidate regardless of timeline is the reason pieces end up getting donated at the last minute.

Timeline What Actually Works
8+ weeks Marketplace is viable if you list now, price honestly, and have a recognized brand
4–8 weeks Marketplace becomes a risk; a direct offer provides timing certainty
2–4 weeks Direct offer is the clear choice for quality pieces; donation for everything else
Under 2 weeks Direct offer only if the piece qualifies; otherwise donate

If your move is within the next 4–6 weeks, it’s worth getting a direct offer before committing to a listing. Submit photos and get a firm offer within 24 hours — so you know exactly what you’re choosing between.

List While You’re Still Using the Piece — Then Lock It In

The most effective thing a seller can do is list before the packing begins.

A piece photographed in your actual living room — with furniture around it, natural light, real context — converts better than a piece in an empty room or a storage unit. Buyers commit faster when they can see how a piece lives. You also have flexibility to schedule pickup on your timeline, not around a panic. The piece can sell three weeks before your move with pickup the day before you leave.

If You Have Multiple Pieces, Sequence Them Early

Not every piece deserves the same strategy — and the sellers who end up donating four items in the final 48 hours made the same mistake: they didn’t decide early enough.

Start with your highest-value pieces — recognized brands, solid condition. These are worth the most and also the most likely to qualify for a faster resolution. Commit to a channel for these first, before anything else.

Mid-tier pieces go on Marketplace immediately if that’s the route. Not when you’re ready — now. Every week of delay is a week of potential buyer search time you’re giving up, and you don’t have weeks to spare.

Pieces that won’t sell at meaningful value should be routed to donation early. Habitat for Humanity ReStore takes scheduled pickups, but their calendar fills fast during moving season. If you wait until two weeks before your move to call, you may find they’re fully booked — which means those pieces end up being donated in a rush or paying for junk removal.

Decide the channel for each piece in week one. That single decision eliminates most of the chaos that comes later.

What Qualifies for a Direct Offer

Not everything qualifies, and being clear about this upfront saves everyone time.

Edmonton Refreshed buys from recognized manufacturers: B&B Italia, Natuzzi, Rove Concepts, Crate & Barrel, EQ3, West Elm, American Leather, and similar brands that hold real resale value. Structural condition needs to be solid — no sagging frames, no water or smoke damage, no significant pet damage. Neutral colourways move faster and receive better offers.

Pickup covers Edmonton and the surrounding metro: Sherwood Park, St. Albert, Spruce Grove, Fort Saskatchewan, Leduc — same terms, same timeline.

The process is straightforward — and more importantly, predictable. Submit photos, receive a firm offer within 24 hours, and schedule pickup around your move date. No back-and-forth. No coordinating with buyers who need to find a truck. No uncertainty about whether it will actually be gone in time.

If your piece qualifies, this is the option that removes the variable entirely.

The Tradeoff Is Time and Effort, Not Just Price

Marketplace will often produce a higher number than a direct offer. That’s true. What Marketplace doesn’t show you is everything that goes into reaching that number: the listing, the photos, the messages, the no-shows, the coordination, the week it costs you when the buyer falls through and you start over.

The difference between channels isn’t just what you receive — it’s what you spend to get there. Time is the cost that doesn’t show up on the comparison until after the fact, and under a fixed move date, it’s the one cost you can’t absorb.

Marketplace can work — but it requires more time, coordination, and uncertainty than most sellers want once they’re in the middle of managing a move.

Donation Is a Decision, Not a Last Resort

Donation gets treated as giving up. It isn’t. A piece donated on your timeline, with a scheduled pickup and a tax receipt, is a better outcome than the same piece donated in a panic on moving day.

If a piece doesn’t qualify for a direct buyer and you don’t want to run the full Marketplace process, making the donation decision early is the right move — not the fallback. In week two of an eight-week window, that’s a clean decision. In the final 48 hours, it’s the only option you have left.

Decide early. Execute cleanly. The mental overhead of a lingering “maybe I’ll list it” costs more than the decision is worth.

Frequently Asked Questions

How far in advance should I list furniture before moving?

At least 6–8 weeks for Marketplace, especially for larger pieces. Most quality pieces take 3–6 weeks to sell privately, and you still need time to coordinate pickup after a sale is agreed. Listings started 2–3 weeks before a move regularly don’t close before moving day — which means the piece either goes with you or gets donated at the last minute.

Is it better to sell before or after moving in Edmonton?

Before, in most cases. Selling from the current location means buyers can see the piece in context, pickup logistics are known, and you’re not paying to move something you’ll sell immediately after. The exception: if your timeline is too short for Marketplace and the piece doesn’t qualify for a direct buyer, a clean donation before the move is better than a rushed sale you can’t close in time.

What is the real cost of moving a sectional instead of selling it?

Higher than most people account for. Professional moving crews in Edmonton charge by the hour, and a large sectional typically takes 1.5–3 hours depending on distance and difficulty — before you factor in whether it will fit in the new space. Run the actual comparison between selling now and moving it; the gap is often smaller than sellers expect until they do the math.

How fast can Edmonton Refreshed pick up furniture before a move?

Typically within 24–48 hours of an offer being accepted. Mention your move date when you submit photos — accommodating seller timelines is standard. Pickup is handled with truck and labour; you confirm the appointment, and the piece is gone.

Moving soon? The furniture doesn’t have to be a problem. If you have time, energy, and flexibility, Marketplace can work. But if your move date is fixed and you want certainty, the simplest path is to remove the variable entirely. Get an offer, schedule pickup, and move on.

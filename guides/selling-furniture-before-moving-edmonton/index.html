Moving Guide

How to Sell Furniture Before a Move in Edmonton (Without Running Out of Time)

Edmonton Refreshed · Collin Bottrell

Selling furniture before a move goes smoothly when you decide early what comes with you, choose a route for everything else, and work back from the date it all has to be out. Whether you’re moving across Edmonton or downsizing to a smaller home, the steps are the same, and there are good options at every stage, including when moving day is close.

Start With Two Dates

The date it has to be gone. This is a hard deadline someone else sets: your possession date, the end of your lease, the day the movers arrive, or your building’s elevator booking.

The date you’d like it gone. This is your preference, usually a few days before the hard deadline so packing and cleaning aren’t happening around a sofa.

Give both dates to anyone collecting a piece, whether that’s a buyer, a store, or a charity. A preferred date can move; a possession deadline can’t, and the person arranging pickup needs to know which is which.

Decide What’s Worth Moving

Go room by room and ask four questions of each large piece:

  • Will it fit? Measure the rooms in the new home, and the doorways, stairwells, and elevator it has to pass through. A sectional that suits the old living room may not suit the new one.
  • What condition is it in? If you’d replace it within a year or two anyway, moving it only delays the decision.
  • What would replacing it cost? Compare that with the moving cost below, whatever the piece cost new: one that fits and works may cost little to move and much more to replace.
  • What does moving it add? Movers usually charge by time or volume. Ask for a quote with and without your largest pieces.

Our guide to deciding what to keep, sell, or replace before a move goes through this in more detail.

Where to Sell Used Furniture Before a Move in Edmonton

For each piece you’re not taking, pick the route that suits the item, its condition, and the time you have.

RouteSuitsTime needed
Private salePieces in good condition that buyers search forThe most, since you find the buyer
ConsignmentPieces a store agrees to takeThe store sells on its own schedule
DonationUsable pieces a charity acceptsDepends on the charity’s pickup or drop-off times
Giving it awayUsable pieces with little resale valueOften quick
DisposalWorn-out or damaged piecesQuick, but usually costs something

Private sale. Facebook Marketplace and Kijiji reach the most local buyers and can pay the most. You handle the messages, viewings, and price talks, and the buyer arranges the move.

Consignment. A store sells the piece for you and keeps a share of the price, often marking it down the longer it sits. You get paid after it sells, which may be after you’ve moved. Before you consign, read the agreement for who delivers the piece, the commission, the markdown schedule, and when you can withdraw it or change its price; once a store has accepted it, you may not be free to do either on your own timeline. Our consignment and resale guide compares the options.

Donation. Charities, including Habitat for Humanity’s ReStore, accept furniture by type and condition, and pickup depends on their schedule and your area. Check what they take and book early.

Giving it away. A free listing, a community group, or a friend can clear a usable piece quickly, especially if you say plainly that the taker needs to collect it.

Disposal. City of Edmonton Eco Stations accept furniture drop-offs; check their accepted items list for fees. The city’s seasonal Big Bin Events take old couches and other worn-out furniture free of charge. If you can’t transport it, book a junk removal service.

What to Gather Before You List

The same information works for a listing, a consignment store, or a donation booking. Gather it once:

  • Photos in daylight: the front, both sides, the back, close-ups of any wear, and the label.
  • Brand and model, from the label or the original order.
  • Dimensions: width, depth, and height, and each piece of a sectional separately.
  • Condition, described plainly, including stains, fading, pet or smoke exposure, and anything that doesn’t work.
  • Location: your neighbourhood or town.
  • Access: stairs, elevator and any booking rules, parking, and whether the piece has to come apart to get out.
  • Your two dates: when you’d like it gone, and when it has to be gone.

Price it for its condition, not for what you paid. Search Marketplace for the same model, or similar pieces, in similar condition, and price to sell before your backup date. A piece that keeps getting relisted is usually priced above what buyers will pay. Our guide to what a used sofa is worth covers pricing in more detail.

Spell out the pickup in the listing. Say where the piece is (main floor, basement, third-floor walk-up), that the buyer needs a vehicle and help, the dates it can be collected, and how you’ll be paid.

How to Sell Furniture Before Moving: Plan Around Your Move Date

The windows below are planning suggestions, not how long a sale will take. Sale times vary with the piece, its price, and the time of year.

If you have more than a month

You have time to try a private listing or consignment. List with good photos and a fair price, and pick a date about two weeks before your hard deadline to review how it’s going.

If the move is a few weeks away

Decide each piece now. A private listing can still work at a price that attracts buyers quickly. If donation is your fallback, confirm now that the charity accepts the piece and when it can pick up.

If the furniture has to go fast

With days left, rely on what you can confirm: a charity that has agreed to take the piece, a booked junk removal, or a City drop-off you can drive to yourself. A free listing can clear a piece quickly, but it still depends on someone coming to collect it, so give it a deadline and keep a confirmed option behind it. If you still want to sell, price the piece to move and offer flexible pickup times.

Plan the Handoff

  • Keep what you need. Sell your bed, sofa, or table with a pickup date near the move rather than living without them for weeks. Say the pickup date in the listing.
  • Make the pickup easy. Book the elevator if your building requires it, clear a path, remove legs or cushions that come off, and tell the buyer about parking and stairs.
  • Get paid before it leaves. Cash or an e-transfer that has arrived, not a promise to pay later.
  • Keep a second contact. If another buyer was interested, keep their details until the first sale is done.

Set a backup date

Choose the date you’ll change course if a piece hasn’t sold, and write down what you’ll do: lower the price, switch to donation, or give it away. Deciding in advance means the decision gets made on time instead of the night before the movers arrive. Confirm the fallback before you rely on it, too: a charity that hasn’t agreed to take the piece, or a buyer who hasn’t paid, isn’t a plan yet. A piece on consignment follows the store’s agreement, so check its withdrawal terms before you count on changing course.

Frequently Asked Questions

How far in advance should I start selling furniture before moving?

Start as soon as you know your move date. If you want to try a private listing or consignment, a month or more before the date the piece has to leave gives you time to adjust the price and still fall back on another route. That’s a planning suggestion, not how long a sale will take; sale times vary with the piece, its price, and the time of year.

Is it better to sell furniture before or after moving?

Usually before. Selling from your current home avoids paying to move a piece you don’t plan to keep, and buyers can see it set up. Moving it makes sense if it fits the new place and you want to keep it; measure the new rooms and doorways before you decide.

Should I move my sectional or sell it?

Measure the new space, including doorways, stairs, and elevators, and ask your movers for a quote with and without the sectional. If it won’t fit or you’d replace it soon anyway, selling it before the move usually makes more sense than moving it and selling later.

What’s the difference between a preferred pickup date and a possession deadline?

A preferred pickup date is when you’d like the piece gone, and it can move. A possession deadline is when it has to be gone, set by your sale, lease, movers, or building, and it can’t. Give both to anyone collecting the piece so they know which date is fixed.

What can I do with furniture nobody wants to buy before a move?

Donate it if a charity accepts it, or give it away through a free listing or community group, keeping a confirmed option in case nobody collects it. For worn-out pieces, City of Edmonton Eco Stations accept furniture drop-offs (fees vary by item), and the city’s seasonal Big Bin Events take old couches and other worn-out furniture free of charge. A junk removal service is the paid option if you can’t transport it.

Where can I donate furniture before moving in Edmonton?

Charities such as Habitat for Humanity’s ReStore accept furniture donations, but each accepts items by type and condition, and pickup depends on their schedule and your area. Confirm they’ll take the piece and book the date before you rely on it.

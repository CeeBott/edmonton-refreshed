Buying a Used Sofa or Sectional in Edmonton: Private Seller or Furniture Reseller?

Edmonton Refreshed · Collin Bottrell

Facebook Marketplace is a platform, not a type of seller. Private owners list their own furniture there, and so do furniture businesses, including us. So the real choice is between buying from a private owner and buying from a business that selects, prepares, and resells used furniture, whether you find the listing on Marketplace, Kijiji, a store’s website, or a used furniture store’s floor.

Either can be a good way to buy a sofa. This guide compares what each typically includes and what to confirm with any seller.

What Each Option Typically Includes

Practices vary from seller to seller on both sides, so treat this as what to expect and ask about, not what you’ll get.

Condition informationWhat the owner knows from living with itMay include a written description or gradeClose-ups of wear, the label, its age, and any repairs
Cleaning and preparationVaries; ask what has been doneVariesWhat was cleaned or repaired, and how

Collection or deliveryUsually you collect itDelivery is often available, usually for a feeThe cost, the timing, and who carries it in

A private owner can give you excellent information, and a reseller’s description is only as good as the work behind it. Ask the same questions of both. Our guide to buying a used sofa covers what to ask and what to check in person.

What a Private Owner Can Tell You, and What a Reseller Can

Each kind of seller knows different things about a piece, so ask each the questions its position can answer.

  • A private owner knows its history: when and where it was bought, how it was used, whether there were pets or smoking in the home, what has been cleaned or repaired, and why they’re selling. An owner may not have looked underneath it, checked the label for the model, or compared its condition against any standard, so “good condition” is their own judgment.
  • A reseller may have inspected, cleaned, and documented the piece, and may give it a condition grade. It usually knows less about the piece’s life before it arrived, so ask what it learned from the previous owner as well as what it checked itself.

Our guide to what condition grades mean can help you compare one seller’s description with another’s.

Is It Cheaper to Buy a Used Sofa on Marketplace? Compare the Total Cost

Compare the complete offer for each piece you’re considering, not just the asking price. Add up the same lines for each, and keep your time and effort separate from the cash total: they’re real, but they’re not cash.

What to add upWhat to includeWhat to check
Purchase priceThe price you agree, plus any tax that appliesWhether the seller’s price includes tax
Getting it homeA delivery fee, or a truck or van rental and at least two people to move it; a sectional may need moreStairs, distance, and who carries it in at each end
Work still neededCleaning, new cushion inserts, or a repairA quote for anything you’d have done
Already includedInspection, cleaning, delivery, or other preparation the seller providesLeave these out of the other lines so you don’t count them twice
Time and effortSearching, messaging, viewing, and arranging pickup, sometimes across several listingsWeigh it separately from the cash total

Either option can cost less overall, depending on the piece and what the seller provides. A business’s price may be higher and still be good value to you if it includes things you’d otherwise do or go without: an inspection, cleaning, a written description of condition, somewhere to see and sit on the piece, or delivery arranged for you. A private sale can be the better buy when the piece is in good order and you can collect it yourself. Whichever you’re considering, compare the price with similar pieces in similar condition and with the current price of the same model new; our guide to sectional sofa cost shows how.

Reading a Private Asking Price

A private seller’s price usually reflects what they paid, how soon they need the piece gone, and the listings they’ve seen, so two similar pieces can be priced very differently. A listing rarely says the original price, the exact model, or how the seller weighed its condition. Ask, then compare it with similar pieces in similar condition. Our guide to what a used sofa is worth covers how to weigh comparable listings by model, condition, and terms. With any seller, private or business, establish what the price includes, the piece’s documented condition and any work it needs, how it gets to you and what that costs, and how the complete offer compares with the alternatives you’re considering.

Photos and Materials

Listing photos show what the seller chose to show, in their lighting, and some things rarely come through in them:

  • Stains and discolouration, which can read as shadows in soft light.
  • Pilling and wear on textured fabrics such as bouclé, which a wide shot can hide.
  • Cushion support, which a photo can’t show at all.
  • Wear at the arm tops and seat fronts, especially on leather, when the photos are taken from across the room.

Whoever is selling, ask for close-ups of the seat fronts, arm tops, and any marks, and a photo of the label. See the piece in person before you pay where that’s practical. If you’re buying from a distance, ask for detailed photos and a video walkthrough; they can show condition, but not how it feels to sit on or how it smells.

“Leather” in a listing can mean different things. Bonded leather is a composite made from leather fibres with a synthetic surface, and it can peel or crack. Genuine leather can also crack, dry out, or fade with age and use, so check its condition either way. Our guide to leather sofas explains the terms.

What to Confirm With Any Seller

  • The brand and model, from the label or the receipt.
  • The dimensions, and for a sectional, each section’s size and which side the chaise is on as you face it. Our guide to measuring for a sectional covers your room and the route in.
  • Its condition, including what has been cleaned or repaired.
  • The final price after you’ve seen it, what it includes, and when you’ll pay.
  • How it gets to you, and what that costs.
  • Any return terms, in writing, before you pay.

Which Suits You

Buying from a private owner can suit you if you find the right piece, are comfortable inspecting it yourself, and can arrange transport. It can be a thoughtful choice for a main living-room sofa as much as a spare one.

Buying from a reseller can suit you if you value documented condition, preparation before sale, and delivery being available. What you’re paying for is the work that business actually does and records, so ask what that is.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I trust furniture photos on Facebook Marketplace?

Listing photos show what the seller chose to show, in their lighting, whether the seller is a private owner or a business. Ask for close-ups of the seat fronts, arm tops, any marks, and the label. See the piece in person before you pay where that’s practical, or ask for detailed photos and a video walkthrough if you’re buying from a distance, keeping in mind that they can’t show comfort or smell.

Is it cheaper to buy a sofa on Facebook Marketplace?

The asking price is often lower from a private owner, but compare the total: the price, the cost of a vehicle and help to get it home, and any cleaning or repair it needs, alongside a reseller’s price with whatever cleaning and delivery it includes. Either can come out cheaper depending on the piece.

Do furniture resellers charge more than private sellers?

A reseller’s asking price is often higher, partly because it may include cleaning or other preparation. Compare the total for each option: the purchase price, the cost of getting it home, and any cleaning or repair it needs.

Is buying from a reseller different from buying as-is on Marketplace?

It depends on the seller. Both private owners and resellers may sell as-is. What differs is how much condition information, preparation, and delivery each offers, so ask, and get any return terms in writing before you pay.

How much does delivery cost?

Private sellers usually expect you to collect. Resellers set their own delivery fees. Edmonton Refreshed delivers across Edmonton and the surrounding area for an additional fee, typically within a few days of purchase; ask us for the cost for the piece you’re considering.

Where can I buy a used sofa in Edmonton?

From private owners on Facebook Marketplace and Kijiji, and from businesses that resell used furniture, which may list on those platforms, on their own websites, or in a store. Whichever you choose, ask the same questions about condition, preparation, delivery, and returns.

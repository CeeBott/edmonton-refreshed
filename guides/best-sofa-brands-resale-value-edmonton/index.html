Which Sofa Brands Hold Their Value? What We’ve Seen in Edmonton

Edmonton Refreshed · Collin Bottrell

“Holds its value” can mean three different things, and a sofa can do well on one and poorly on another. This guide separates them, explains what affects each, and shares what we’ve seen in pieces we’ve bought and sold. These are observations from our own pieces, not a ranking of brands across the Edmonton market.

Three Ways to Measure Resale Value

  • The dollar amount it sells for. A sofa that cost a great deal new can sell for more dollars used than a modest one, even after losing much of its price.
  • The share of its original price it keeps. That depends on what it cost new as much as on what it sells for used, so a very expensive piece doesn’t necessarily keep a larger share.
  • How readily it attracts a suitable buyer. A familiar model in a common size can suit many buyers; a large, expensive, or distinctive piece may suit a smaller group who are looking for it specifically.

Construction quality affects how a sofa holds up, which affects its condition when you sell. But a well-built sofa isn’t automatically easy to sell or worth a high share of what it cost.

What Affects What a Used Sofa Sells For

  • Whether it can be identified. A label or receipt naming the maker and model lets buyers look it up and compare it with similar listings.
  • Its condition, including wear, fading, odour, and anything that needs repair.
  • Its size and configuration, and how many homes it suits.
  • Its upholstery and colour, and whether they suit what buyers are looking for.
  • How recognizable the brand is. A well-known name makes a piece easier to identify and compare. Limited recognition makes that harder, but it doesn’t mean the piece is poorly built or has no resale value.
  • How many similar pieces are for sale at the same time.

Pieces We’ve Handled

Natuzzi Editions Cesare. A twelve-year-old leather sectional that had sat nearly unused in a basement. Once cleaned and conditioned, the hide and seats came back like new, apart from a few very small marks, and we graded it Good. Its age mattered less than how little it had been used. Our Natuzzi guide covers the brand in more detail.

American Leather Tuscany. About fourteen years old and previously under-cared-for. Cleaning and conditioning brought the leather back well, but very minor surface cracking remained, and we graded it Fair. How a piece has been cared for shows up in its condition, whatever the brand.

Crate & Barrel Rochelle. A sofa and matching chair, structurally solid and cosmetically clean but significantly sun-faded, which we disclosed to the buyer. We graded it Fair. Fading counts against condition regardless of how well a piece is built.

Crate & Barrel Gather. A 99-inch deep sofa in Tribute performance fabric, made in May 2024 and sold to us about a year later with no visible wear, graded Excellent. Knowing the model and fabric by name made it straightforward to compare with current listings. Our Crate & Barrel guide covers how to find the model on a label.

Brentwood Classics Dorado. A Canadian-made sofa in aniline leather, graded Excellent. Brentwood Classics isn’t as widely recognized as some names on this list, so describing the model and the leather by name did much of the work of showing buyers what it was.

Rove Concepts Milo. A six-piece modular sectional in bouclé, graded Excellent. Its modules can be arranged more than one way, which we showed in the listing photos. Rove Concepts, which has a showroom in Vancouver, designs the Milo; our Rove Concepts guide covers the brand.

B&B Italia Charles. A sectional we currently have listed, from a maker whose new prices run far above most sofas’. A piece like this suits a specialist group of buyers who know the design and are looking for it. That says something about its buyer pool, not that luxury pieces sell more easily. Our B&B Italia guide covers the brand.

Construction Belongs to the Model, Not the Brand

Frames, suspension, cushions, and upholstery vary from model to model, even within one brand. To judge a piece, find the model’s published specification and check the piece’s present condition.

  • Frames: solid hardwood, plywood, and steel can all be built well or badly. “Kiln-dried” means the wood was dried to a controlled moisture content, which helps reduce later shrinkage and movement; it doesn’t on its own describe how a frame was designed or joined. Our guide to judging a sofa’s quality covers the checks.
  • Suspension: sinuous springs, coil springs, and webbing systems differ in design and quality from one maker and model to the next. Check the specification for the model rather than assuming one from the brand.
  • Leather: the hide layer (full-grain, top-grain, split), the surface treatment, and the finish (aniline, semi-aniline, pigmented) are separate attributes. Bonded leather is a composite, not a grade. Our guide to leather sofas explains each.
  • Fabric: polyester and blends vary widely in weave and durability, and “performance fabric” is the maker’s claim that a fabric resists stains or cleans easily. Our guide to fabric sofas covers what the labels mean.

Brands You’ll Come Across

Natuzzi, B&B Italia, Rove Concepts, EQ3, Crate & Barrel, Pottery Barn, West Elm, CB2, Article, Brentwood Classics, American Leather, and Urban Barn all come up in Edmonton’s used market, alongside Structube, IKEA, Ashley, and many brands buyers won’t recognize. Each sells a range of models at different prices and with different construction, so a brand name tells you where to start looking, not what a particular piece is. Our EQ3 and Pottery Barn guides, along with the brand guides above, cover what to look for from specific makers.

Working Out the Cost of Owning a Sofa

Resale value is one part of what a sofa costs you over the years you own it:

(Purchase price + delivery + any repairs − what you sell it for) ÷ the years you own it

A hypothetical example. A $3,500 sofa kept for ten years and sold for $500 costs ($3,500 − $500) ÷ 10 = $300 a year. A $1,200 sofa kept for four years and then given away costs $1,200 ÷ 4 = $300 a year. With these assumptions they cost the same per year; change how long you keep each one or what it sells for, and the result changes. The figures are for illustration only.

Practical Advice

If you’re buying, choose a sofa that fits your room and how you live, and inspect the particular piece. Treat possible resale value as a secondary consideration. Our guide to buying a used sofa covers what to check.

If you’re selling, identify the model from the label or receipt, document its condition honestly with clear photos, compare it with listings for the same or similar models, and choose a price for your timeline. Our guide to pricing a used sofa walks through it. If it’s a premium sofa or sectional, you can also ask us for an offer; that offer is what our business can pay for that piece, not a valuation of it.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which sofa brands have the best resale value?

It depends what you mean by resale value: the dollar amount a sofa sells for, the share of its original price it keeps, or how readily it finds a suitable buyer. These can point to different pieces. In the pieces we’ve handled, the model, its condition, and whether it can be identified have mattered more than the brand alone. Recognized names such as Natuzzi, Crate & Barrel, EQ3, and Rove Concepts are easier for buyers to identify and compare.

How do EQ3 and CB2 compare?

Both sell a range of models at different prices and with different construction, so neither can be judged as a whole. Compare the specific models you’re considering: the maker’s published specifications, the piece’s condition, and listings for the same or similar models.

What does kiln-dried hardwood mean?

It means the wood was dried in a kiln to a controlled moisture content, which helps reduce later shrinkage and movement. It doesn’t by itself mean a solid-wood frame, and it doesn’t describe how well the frame was designed or joined, so check the model’s specification and the piece’s condition too.

Is a used premium-brand sofa better than a new budget one?

It can be, if the particular piece fits your space, is comfortable, and is in good condition at a total cost you’re happy with. A premium name doesn’t guarantee that, and a less expensive sofa in good condition may suit you well. Compare the specific pieces.

Do leather sofas hold their value better than fabric?

Not as a rule. It depends on the specific piece: the type of leather or fabric, its condition, and whether buyers can identify it. Bonded leather is a composite that tends to peel, which counts against condition, and fading, staining, or cracking count against any upholstery.
